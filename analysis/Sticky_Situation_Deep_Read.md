# Before You Start

This is a full analysis of *Tales of Being Limitless: Sticky Situation*, from the Prologue through Section 4, Chapter 6 ("Curtain Call"). I read the whole manuscript in order and took notes chapter by chapter. Then I went back through it with targeted searches: every eye color, every speed figure, every time a hand curls, every "we're not friends," every dialogue callback. Where I quote the book, the quotes are copied from your text.

The book isn't finished, and I've treated it that way. "Curtain Call" is a cliffhanger, not an ending. Nothing here faults the book for leaving threads open. Where a thread is open, I've filed it under open threads, theories, or predictions.

**How this document is organized**

- **The Verdict.** My overall opinion, a scorecard, and what the book is really about.
- **The Book by the Numbers.** Word counts, structure, pacing, and the timeline I reconstructed.
- **Chapter by Chapter.** All 53 chapters plus the Prologue. Each one gets what happens, the small details I noticed, what the chapter is doing, what works, what I'd push on, and a score out of 10.
- **Character Dossiers.** Every named character and some unnamed ones, with powers, arcs, key lines, relationships, concerns, and predictions.
- **Themes.** The ideas the book keeps returning to.
- **Motifs and Callbacks.** Recurring objects and images, and every setup and payoff I could find.
- **Continuity Ledger.** What holds up, what wobbles, and what's still unexplained.
- **Theories and Predictions.** Ranked by how confident I am, with the evidence for each.
- **Craft Notes.** Prose, dialogue, POV, pacing, and specific line-level habits, with numbers.
- **Closing Verdict.** Best moments, best lines, and what I'd want next.

A note on scores: they're relative to this book. A 6 isn't a bad chapter. It's a chapter doing less than the book's best chapters do. I'm giving you real opinions, because you asked for honesty and because the book can take it.

# The Verdict

## The short version

*Sticky Situation* is a superhero-school story that keeps turning into a story about guilt, being seen, and what it costs to be cheered. The premise looks familiar on purpose: one hero academy, a lazy prodigy, a rival speedster, a healer, a demon boy, a tournament. Then the book spends 100,000 words checking every piece of that setup for rot. The number-one hero is a merch salesman. The tournament is a TV product. The villains offer the mentorship the school never gave. The healer dies. The protagonist survives only because a monster rebuilt his body, and the rebuild is slowly making him crueler.

That's a strong spine. Underneath the jokes, the shipping, the cowboy slang, and the glue puns, the book knows what it's about, and it keeps coming back to that.

The engine is **Kage Ozul**, and he's the book's best decision. His power is adhesive. He calls himself Sticky Situation so that the people he saves will laugh. He's an orphan who "stopped unpacking his bag." His founding trauma is a hand that held on and then held nothing. A boy whose power is attachment, who can't attach, whose great failure was failing to hold on: that's a premise where the power, the wound, and the theme are the same thing. Most superhero fiction never gets there.

## What the book does best

- **Setups and payoffs.** This is the book's biggest strength. The gold glitter in Abby's backpack becomes the whistle. The anonymous maid-outfit photo becomes Inazuma. "Another speedster, huh?" becomes Inazuma again. "Eat breakfast" becomes "I remembered to eat breakfast!" The claymore Kage touches becomes Elias's suspicion, which becomes Lucifer's memory. "Lose my number" becomes "Keep my number." "That name is terrible, pick anything else" and "I'm not yelling that in a crisis" come back word for word with the speakers swapped. Severine's "come to me when you are certain you are significantly superior" comes back over a cup of tea. The book rewards rereading.
- **Fights built like puzzles.** Almost every fight is decided by understanding a rule, not by raw power. Lucifer pushes Severine's half-dome barrier across the ground. Geneki loses by breaking the floor and touching underneath it. Kage beats Elias because excretion counts as an involuntary process. Kage beats Abaddon by choking him instead of hurting him. These are good fight scenes, and they're good because the powers have real costs and limits.
- **Moral mirrors.** Anubis and Thanatos are both death gods and both "mentors" who treat Kage as an asset. Huoyan and Frostbite, fire and ice, are the gruff ones who actually care. Lance is the villain's son who chose heroism, and Kage is the hero student drifting toward villainy. Lucifer and Kage each carry a monster inside them. The cast is built in pairs, and the pairs argue with each other.
- **Big emotional set pieces.** Abby's death. Orion's count-to-five in the shadow dome. Kage's "King Kage" speech. Lance's ants-and-frog story. Kage sticking his tongue out at Alex in the middle of being destroyed. Orion alone, raising the whistle. These land, and they land because the book earned them earlier.

## What holds it back

- **The prose is louder than it needs to be.** There are 2,119 exclamation points in about 105,000 words, roughly one every 50 words. The announcer-style power introductions earn theirs; that's a genre device and a good one. But exclamation points and all-caps dialogue spread into ordinary narration and conversation, and when everything is shouted, the important shouts get lost.
- **Mechanics.** I counted 54 places where a dialogue tag follows a period instead of a comma (`"No, you won't." Anubis answered`). There's a lot of beat repetition: "nodded" appears 82 times and "chuckled" 45. These are easy fixes, and fixing them would make the writing feel noticeably more professional.
- **Emotions get named after they've already been shown.** The book often shows a feeling well and then states it in narration right afterward. Trusting the first version would make the strongest scenes hit harder.
- **The first round of the tournament is a deliberate anticlimax.** Seven of the eight first-round matches end in seconds, by forfeit, fake trip, or ring-out. The book knows this. Inazuma and Anubis both complain about it in-story, and the joke serves the theme of a spectacle that isn't delivering. But eight chapters of this is a risk, and it only works because the scenes between fights are carrying it.

## What the book is really about

The question under every chapter: **Is a hero someone who saves people, or someone people watch?**

Anubis answers *watched*. He wants cameras in the dorms, sells the tournament on TV, loves Kage's story because "an orphaned failure turned number one hero just sells itself," and bets Kage's future on a deal with Lucifer for better ratings. Nerezza, the most sadistic villain, rewatches Kage's screams on her phone because "children screaming in pain is next level television." Put those two lines side by side and the book's thesis is clear: the hero establishment and the cruelest villain are consuming the same thing.

Kage's first answer is *saves*. His hero name exists so that a rescued person will laugh: "A name that can turn something horrifying into something funny." He acts on that philosophy exactly once, at his lowest point, when he sticks out his tongue at Alex in the stands while a ten-foot demon slams him into the floor. That's the purest heroism in the book.

But Kage wants to be watched too. He wants the crowd "forever." He bolts to be first through the stadium doors. And the biomass inside him is turning his need to be seen into a need to win and dominate. The tragedy the book is setting up is a boy whose heroism comes from comforting people and whose hunger comes from wanting to be looked at, with something inside him feeding the hunger.

That's a story worth finishing.

## How the sections are built

Each section is named after a person, except the third, which is named after an event. My reading is that each section is named for whoever holds Kage in place during that stretch:

- **Orion Vance**: the rival who gives Kage a direction. Section 1 is about Kage finding something to push against.
- **Abby Neosame**: the supporter whose loss breaks him. Section 2 is about what happens when the support is gone.
- **Elite Tournament**: the crowd. Section 3 is about Kage being watched, and the watching becomes a character.
- **Ailani Shoma**: the person who knew him before he changed. Section 4 is about someone who can tell whether he's still himself.

There's another structural rhyme I really like. **The two sections named after girls both open with a first-year flashback built around that girl.** Section 2 opens with Abby approaching Lucifer in her second week of school. Section 4 opens (after the interlude) with Ailani meeting Kage over honey toast. Each flashback shows the girl at her most herself, right before the present tests what that self has become. If this is deliberate, keep it. If it isn't, make it deliberate.

## Cast size and the class roster

The Prologue says only **twenty** applicants get selected. Here's everyone I can place in Kage's year of the hero course:

Azucre (Rebento), Bandalore (Cheyenne), Cruz (Reina), Faisceau (Lance), Gardner (Geneki), Gardner (Rhatella), Lund (Alexander), Minkowski (Elias), Nasser (Youseff), Neant (Severine), Neosame (Abby), Ozul (Kage), Salyangozu (Volkan), Shifo (Zulfiya), Shoma (Ailani), Sokudo (Inazuma), Tulivalo (Lucifer), Vance (Orion).

That's **18**. Kelly Iasacht's son, who "didn't end up showing up," is probably the 19th. That leaves **one classmate we haven't met**, if the class is still twenty. That could be nothing. It could also be the most useful empty chair in the book.

The roster also confirms a lovely detail. Rhatella builds gear **in alphabetical order by last name** and says she's on "O." Every Rhatella-made item in the tournament belongs to someone earlier in the alphabet: Azucre's gumball machines, Cruz's heat pack, Faisceau's hilt, Minkowski's glasses. Nobody after "O" has her gear, including Lucifer (T) and Orion (V). That's continuity most writers wouldn't think to keep.


# Chapter by Chapter: Prologue and Section 1, "Orion Vance"

## Prologue

*About 1,050 words. Worldbuilding, then Kage's backstory, then the store.*

**What happens.** Everyone on Earth is born with an enhancement. Heroes get sponsors and cameras. Villains get "erased from the picture." The UN allows exactly one hero program, in one American high school, and keeps putting off expansion for reasons "no one's sure" of. Kage Ozul, an orphan from Japan, joins for the food and the locked door. He drifts through his first year. At the start of his second year a villain attacks his favorite convenience store, and the person whose wrist he's holding is crushed under the awning. Since then he trains until his hands split.

**Details I caught.**

- "Some freak mutation dressed up as destiny." The first sentence of worldbuilding already doubts the premise.
- "In many young minds, it's the same game…they just have different costumes." This is the book's thesis in one line, planted on page one. Thanatos and Anubis are both coming.
- "Kids get scouted young, polished into smiling weapons with brand logos on their chests." This phrase comes back almost word for word in S1 Ch12, when Kage takes Thanatos's hand while thinking of heroes as "weapons who smiled because it was good for the branding."
- **The UN mystery.** "No one's sure why the United Nations only wants one program in one school." This is never explained. Later we learn Anubis needs UN approval "every single time" he buys a drone. Keep watching this.
- "For a kid in Japan named Kage Ozul, it was just a bed that didn't move every month."
- "You don't hang posters if you're not sure how long the walls are yours." This is the best single sentence in the Prologue.
- Thousands of applicants, **twenty** selected.
- "An enhancement he refused to explain." Why refuse? Adhesive isn't shameful. I think this is a deliberate seed. See Theories.
- Teachers said "potential" "like a prayer."
- **"The windows went white and burst outward."** Read that again after you meet Frostbite. White windows bursting outward is what frost does to glass.
- "His fingers were holding air." This is the grip motif in its first form, and the whole book grows out of it.
- "Adhering to every rule—well, most of the time." An adhesive pun hidden in the narration. The book does this kind of thing quietly and often.

**What it's doing.** It does three jobs efficiently: the world's cynicism, Kage's poverty and detachment, and the wound. It ends on the book's central question: "Is he becoming the kind of hero who saves people, or someone just desperate not to lose somebody like that again?"

**What works.** This is the most controlled prose in the manuscript. The sentences are varied, the images are concrete, and the rhythm is literary. "You don't hang posters…" and "his fingers were holding air" do more character work in eight words than some chapters do in a page.

**What I'd push on.** The tonal gap between this Prologue and Chapter 1 is wide. The Prologue reads like a literary novel. Chapter 1 reads like anime dialogue with exclamation-point power cards. Both voices are good, but the shift is abrupt. You could either bring a little of the Prologue's restraint into the chapters, or bring a little of the chapters' bounce into the Prologue's last paragraph so the reader is ready for the switch.

**Score: 9/10.** A strong opening that plants more than it seems to.

## 1.1 Nice To Meet You

*1,177 words. Training field. Present day, shortly after the store.*

**What happens.** Kage punches a bag hard enough to leave knuckle dents. Orion, a classmate he doesn't really know, tells him to take a breather and mentions the store gently. Abby arrives with water bottles from her dolphin backpack and heals Kage's split knuckles. Kage, delighted, proposes a no-holds-barred fight with Orion since Abby can heal them both. Orion agrees to "a spar? With rules? Maybe," and Abby counts it down.

**Details I caught.**

- The first line: "Kage Ozul used to come to the training field to hit on chicks, but now it was where he made his hands bleed." It gives you before, after, and the flaw in one sentence.
- Kage: arctic blue eyes, scarlet hair "drooped in front of his face." The droop matters later. He cuts it off in S2 Ch10 because it was "lazy."
- He punches "like a boxer aiming for someone's vitals." **This exact phrase returns in S2 Ch13 for Orion,** after Orion thinks Kage is dead. Orion takes on Kage's grief-punching. That's one of the best callbacks in the book.
- Orion's gold: rings, loc rings, lip piercings, earrings, necklaces, bracelets. The loc rings become ammunition against Rebento in S4 Ch5.
- Orion already knows Kage "was the one at that store." He's kind about it: "happens to more heroes than you think."
- Abby: yellow eyes, a "twin-layered halo," nearly silent steps, a dolphin-shaped backpack.
- Abby's first words to Kage: "I'm Abby, and your hands?" And then: "**It's okay, Kage. Everything is going to be alright.**" She says these words again as she dies.
- The first power card: "The girl is Abby Neosame! Her enhancement is Eyes of Divinity!" The narration is present-tense and exclamatory, like a broadcast. "Some are worried she may actually have fallen from heaven."
- Abby's limits: the patient must be alive, it doesn't work on herself, and serious wounds need speed. Every one of those limits matters later.
- "Fights need a permit; I'm not breaking rules."
- Kage dodges Orion's question about his enhancement.

**What it's doing.** It introduces the first two members of the trio and the dynamic Kage will spend the book in: Orion grounds him, Abby heals him, and he asks for a fight.

**What works.** Kage's grab for the water bottle as a way to escape talking about the store is a sharp piece of characterization. His instinct is to fight instead of feel, and the chapter shows it.

**What I'd push on.** Orion's first speech is almost a counselor's script ("if you need a breather, take one. Helps more than people know"). It works for who he is, but a rougher edge here would make his later coldness ("We're not friends") feel like a choice rather than a different character.

**Score: 8/10.** Efficient setup, warm, and full of seeds.

## 1.2 Birth Of A Rivalry

*1,855 words. Training field, same scene.*

**What happens.** First spar. Kage opens with tape, then spins and sprays glue. Orion reads him and kicks him across the turf. Kage gets up, rockets forward on glue jets from his toenails, sticks Orion, and lands a big glue blast. Huoyan (Hellfeather) wants to stop it; Anubis says to let them finish. Orion tries to end it with Divine Relay. Kage anchors himself with hardened glue and takes the full hit, coughing blood at 105°F, then grabs Orion's leg and slams them both into the ground before passing out. Abby heals them. Anubis calls them "brilliant." Huoyan issues warnings.

**Details I caught.**

- Kage's card: lipids are fuel, adhesives come out through the **nails**, hardening draws **cold from his body**, and overuse causes fever. These are excellent costs because they're bodily and specific.
- Orion's card: gold and violet, comet trails, "up to two hundred miles per hour," overheating and joint strain, Zephyr Mode. Named techniques: Aether Step and Divine Relay.
- Kage: "**Another** speedster, huh? Ugh, I hate you fast types." Paid off by Inazuma in S3 Ch3.
- "My power is made to slow things down!" That's a thesis for his whole fighting style.
- Orion: "Speed isn't just running straight. It's perception and timing." This becomes a running line, used straight, mocked, and eventually turned back on Orion.
- Kage: "You thought the dude who messed up being a hero would be easy pickings, huh?!" He's projecting. Orion never said that.
- Kage's temperature goes from **102 to 105**, and later to 110 in S2 Ch2.
- "**This time, he made sure his grip held.**" The Prologue wound, healed in miniature, in his first real fight. It's beautiful.
- "You…insane idiot…you nearly had me."
- **Anubis calls Huoyan "Hon."** "C'mon, Hon, admit it." I flagged this on first read. It turns out to be one of the most important threads in the adult cast.
- "He turned as his own enhancement seemed to activate." Anubis's enhancement is never named in the rest of the book. That makes this line a seed.
- Huoyan: "That reflects on me!" His first worry is reputation. That's a fun irony, since he's the one who later attacks Anubis for caring about appearances.
- Abby finished her homework "this morning."

**What it's doing.** It establishes the rivalry as a fight between control (Orion) and stubbornness (Kage), and it puts both adult mentors on stage.

**What works.** The anchoring move is the best beat in Section 1. It's tactical, it's self-destructive, and it ties straight back to the store. The adult reactions do quick characterization: Huoyan is rules and reputation, Anubis is spectacle and "let them finish."

**What I'd push on.** Two tall men "one with pale skin and bright white hair, the other with gray hair and sunglasses" leaves the reader to sort out which is which. It's clear by the end of the book, but one more word ("the white-haired one, her teacher…") would help here.

**Score: 9/10.** The rivalry is born, and so is the motif.

## 1.3 A Beautiful Past

*953 words. Training room one, the next day.*

**What happens.** Kage, trying to impress Anubis, visits training room one for the first time and finds Ailani Shoma, his ex, on a treadmill. She tries to leave. He blocks the door, jokes, and hugs her from behind. A sigil flashes on her hand. She tells him she's done, mentions the eight women he's dated since, and he corrects her: nine. She threatens to switch classes and slams the door.

**Details I caught.**

- He's attracted to her before he recognizes her. That's a nice way to show the pull is physical before it's history.
- Ailani is "Latina, Black, and Japanese mixed."
- "**Kage could almost imagine the smell of fresh-baked bread and honey.**" This is the honey toast from S4 Ch1, planted forty chapters early.
- She'd "rehearsed this a hundred times in her head." She's avoiding him because it still costs her something.
- "Call, DM, text, email, smoke signal, letter in a bottle…?"
- She hides a smile and wipes it away.
- He hugs her "like he could hold on to the version of them that was already gone." This is the grip motif applied to love.
- "Just like how we met, remember?" Paid off in S4 Ch1: they met in a "fight."
- **The sigil** appears and disappears. She's choosing not to use her power on him.
- "Actually…it was nine." It's a funny line and a self-destructive one.
- "Leaving him alone with the hum of machines and everything he hadn't fixed yet."

**What it's doing.** It adds romantic stakes and shows the old Kage through the eyes of someone who knew him.

**What works.** It's short and it hurts. The chapter title is ironic in a good way.

**What I'd push on.** Ailani's reasons ("you did nothing, didn't care about anyone… still flirting") are all stated in one speech. A single specific memory, one moment he failed her, would make the breakup feel lived-in rather than summarized.

**Score: 8/10.** Small chapter, big ache, and a forty-chapter plant.

## 1.4 Rhat Tech

*1,401 words. Rhatella's dorm.*

**What happens.** Orion, enjoying a slow walk, notices a door leaking blue light. Inside is a dorm that's "much, much bigger on the inside," run by an AI called C.O.R.E. that tries to take his blood. C.O.R.E. explains its three objectives and lets him up to the workshop, where he meets Rhatella Gardner: four foot eight, wired, goggled, rat-eared. She builds gear for classmates in alphabetical order and is stuck on "O" for Ozul. Orion declines gear, asks if she has friends, and they fist-bump into friendship.

**Details I caught.**

- Orion's "guilty pleasure" is walking slowly. For a speedster, that's a great detail.
- C.O.R.E. (Centralized Operational Response Engine): androgynous voice, cyan eyes on monitors.
- **The syringe.** It tries to draw Orion's blood; he slaps it away. In S1 Ch9 it succeeds with Kage. Rhatella's lab has a sample of Kage's blood from before the biomass. That could matter a lot.
- "You do not wish to beg my creator for advice or support gear? You are not here to scold her for her creations? That is…new." That tells you how people usually treat her.
- The third objective: "riskier actions" in extreme cases.
- "A model student with no filed record of misconduct." Orion got a verbal warning the day before. Either warnings aren't filed or C.O.R.E.'s records lag. It's minor.
- "I'm on 'O' right now, but GOD, this Ozul kid is driving me crazy! **As a conductor, trying to make an item for a human insulator is the WORST!**" Kage is a human insulator. That's an odd thing to be, and I think it matters. See Theories.
- Every gadget is stamped with a cartoon of her face. This pays off when Kage sees the stamp on Elias's glasses and hesitates before breaking them.
- "As long as I'm useful, right?" This is the saddest line in the chapter.
- "You're officially my longest conversation with a classmate…ever!"
- Orion: "I like doing things on my own. That's the best way to improve, right?" That's Orion's flaw, stated kindly.
- "I wouldn't recommend having 'kinda' friends."

**What it's doing.** It gives Orion his own subplot and one of the book's most quietly sad characters.

**What works.** Rhatella is introduced with real loneliness under the noise. The alphabetical-gear rule is a great device that the book honors all the way through.

**What I'd push on.** C.O.R.E.'s three-objectives speech is a lot of exposition delivered straight at Orion. It's in character for an AI, and the book makes a joke of it later when Kage cuts it off, but you could trim it here.

**Score: 8/10.** Great character introduction; a little speechy.

## 1.5 Stronger Than Strong

*1,722 words. Training room three.*

**What happens.** Kage, respecting Ailani's wishes, uses room three, slams "MAX SETTINGS," and gets dogpiled by thirty robonoids. Abby stops them by voice and heals him. ## 1.6 The Race

## 1.7 Class In Session

*1,952 words. Huoyan's classroom, the following Monday.*

**What happens.** Abby heals Kage's bruise and kisses his cheek. Ailani ignores him. Rhatella and Orion arrive, and Kage and Orion announce "We're not friends!" in unison. Kage explains the rivalry. **Details I caught.**

- Abby "already know[s]" about the bruise. "Why do you think I sat next to you?" Her power scans automatically, which matters for Lucifer later.
- The first "**We're not friends!**" I count at least eight instances of this motif across the book.
- Rhatella hisses and gnaws on Orion's arm: "I KILL FOR THIS RELATIONSHIP DRAMA!"
- Kage on rivalry: "When me and Orion are in competition, we understand each other… He's seven inches taller than me for god's sake!… **He could die tomorrow and I wouldn't really grieve.**" Section 2 tests that sentence hard. Orion believes Kage died, and grieves.
- Abby: "Will I always have to pick one?"

- Lucifer is "the top second-year hero student in the world." Since there's only one program, that's literally true. It's a funny side effect of the worldbuilding.

- "You don't have many friends do you?"

- Orion distracts Huoyan so Lucifer's attack goes unseen. He doesn't do it on purpose, but he stands in the way.

**What it's doing.** It's the full-cast chapter. It sets the classroom map.

He's just been told by Ailani that his flirting cost him everything, and he does it again two days later in front of her. That might be the point, since he's self-sabotaging, but one beat of interiority ("he knew Ailani was ten feet away and did it anyway") would make it a choice instead of a convenience. It matters, because this flirt costs him Ailani again in S2 Ch4.

**Score: 8/10.** A busy chapter that knows where its weight is.

## 1.9 Rhatella In A Novella

*1,351 words. Rhatella's lab, ~2 months later.*

**What happens.** After two more months of losses, Kage seeks out Rhatella. C.O.R.E. scans him and draws his blood. When he cuts off its speech, it kicks him down a shaft with a giant boot. Rhatella, reading a romance novel, panics because his gear isn't done. Kage explains his hero-name philosophy, and she's won over. Orion arrives for a hangout, and he and Kage fight over her time. - Facial, retinal, and fingerprint scans, plus a successful blood draw. "Hey! You better not sell that to anybody!"
- C.O.R.E. is "even more emotionless" with Kage, and later "audibly sighed despite it being an A.I." That's a great joke.
- **Kage's hero-name speech**: "It's kinda like…a name of an action figure a kid could be playing with… Every time I save someone, I want to tell them my name and make them giggle… **A name that can turn something horrifying into something funny.**" This is the moral center of his heroism. **Score: 8.5/10.** The hero-name speech carries it.

## 1.10 Will Without A Way

*1,444 words. The field, Thursday after school.*

**What happens.** Kage attends Huoyan's optional Thursday class, which nobody ever attends. Cheyenne Bandalore shows up because Huoyan wrote it on the board. Huoyan smiles, spreads his wings, and shows them telekinesis. Kage tries to float a glue ball. Cheyenne tells him to relax and he snaps at her. On his last attempt his hair stands on end, the ball floats for a millisecond, and he collapses, still shaking and vibrating.

**Details I caught.**

- Huoyan "enjoyed—nobody ever showing up."
- "Are you trying to catch up to Tulivalo?"
- "Yes, Mr. Niao. I am here to train." Kage being formal is a sign of how much he wants this.
- Cheyenne: unbuttoned blazer, loose red necktie, crimson-and-gold serape, a cowboy hat "she never took off," spurred boots, "the weary intensity of a desert hawk," a strawberry lollipop held like a cigar. It's a vivid entrance.
- The **jingle**: "keys or pocket change." Her power is never shown (see S3 Ch4). I suspect the jingle is a clue.
- The staff forced Huoyan to advertise. That's a small sign of the school's PR pressure.
- "Kage was unaware Huoyan had any personality outside of…dictator."
- Huoyan's card: Phoenix, flaming feathers, **weakness to water**.
- **Telekinesis lore**: "Most people with enhancements that aren't mutations, body alterations like my wings, are capable of doing this. I'm one of the lucky ones… It's like I have two in one." That implies two categories of enhancement, and Huoyan has both.
- Cheyenne: "It's very possible your power can't do telekinesis… I'm pretty sure mine can't." Kage's power (excretion through the nails) sounds like a body-based mutation. So why can he do it at all? See Theories.
- "You don't wanna grab a bull's horns! Calm down, partner."

- "His body continued to shake and vibrate."
- Huoyan: "He should've just failed!… Something's off." His "perfect icy exterior broke."

**What it's doing.** It introduces Cheyenne, cracks Huoyan open, and plants the strongest clue that Kage's power is more than glue.

**What works.** The collapse is genuinely alarming because Huoyan is alarmed. If the strict teacher panics, the reader panics.

**What I'd push on.** The jump from "struggling" to "collapsed" happens in two sentences. One more beat of physical warning (a nosebleed, a ringing in his ears) would make it feel like his body was telling him something.

**Score: 8.5/10.** Quietly one of the most important chapters for the mystery of Kage's power.

## 1.12 Whatever It Takes

*1,759 words. An alley in the city, that night.*

**What happens.** Still exhausted, Kage runs off campus until his legs give out and screams at them. A well-dressed man in an alley introduces himself as Thanatos Shioya, the villain Shifting Horror. He tells Kage that "animals that never stop running don't evolve. They die," and offers to train him "when no one is looking" in exchange for "a few favors." Kage thinks about heroes as branded weapons and shakes his hand.

**Details I caught.**

- **The villain card has no exclamation points.** "This man is the villain Shifting Horror. His enhancement is Bio-Forge." Hero cards are broadcasts. Villain cards are case files. That's one of the smartest stylistic decisions in the book. Keep it everywhere.
- Bio-Forge costs: cells in "overdrive," some inorganic materials "reject integration," and loss of focus brings "migraines, then blackouts." "He is never finished consuming."
- **Kage's hand twitches**, "a twitch in his hand he couldn't quite place." Later his hand curls around nothing whenever Frostbite appears. Bodies remember.
- Thanatos: six feet, a large scar from face to neck, purple eyes, black hair fading to white, black suit and shirt, white tie. He rubs the scar with his thumb.
- "Boy, you seem like your body is going to break any moment now."
- Kage: "I think they call it enhancement. I just call it a power usually."
- "I don't have some power that just cranks my muscle mass up thirty percent. I'm sticky, so I have to make up the difference everywhere else."
- "You speak like someone who's racing toward the top of a tower with no safety line."
- "**You're chasing strength the way a starving animal chases meat, and animals that never stop running don't evolve. They die.**" That's a perfect villain line. It's true, which is why it works.
- "You let curiosity outweigh ambition, even for a moment."
- "Do you want to run past me or run with me?"
- "He always enjoyed watching how ambition bends before it breaks."
- "You're fighting a system that works against everything you naturally are."
- "'Potential man.'"
- "A few favors… nothing that conflicts with what you already want."
- "Weapons who smiled because it was good for the branding." This is the Prologue phrase coming home at the worst possible moment.
- "Kage's heart wasn't on fire, but it beat like he'd turned a page for himself."

**What it's doing.** It's the inciting incident of the book's real plot.

**What works.** Thanatos is persuasive because he's right about everything except what he wants. The chapter makes the handshake feel inevitable instead of stupid, which is hard to do.

**What I'd push on.** The paragraph of Thanatos reading Kage's body ("the micro-tension in his jaw, the tremor under determination, the way ambition propped up exhaustion like splints on a failing structure") is beautiful but stacks three metaphors. Keep the splints and cut one of the others.

**Score: 9.5/10.** The best villain introduction in the book.

## 1.13 Partner, Not Pawn

*1,566 words. The same alley, the next night.*

**What happens.** Thanatos introduces Kage to Frostbite. Frostbite calls Kage a pawn. Kage objects, gets pinned to the wall with ice and punched, then glues Frostbite's mask. Frostbite tries to kill him and Thanatos takes the ice spike through his own shoulder. Thanatos says he won't save Kage again. The task: photograph Anubis's office. After Kage leaves, Thanatos admits Kage is a pawn, and Frostbite mentions "the guy in the chair."

**Details I caught.**

- "Down to the minute." Kage is punctual for villains in a way he never was for school.
- Thanatos pats his head. This is the first paternal gesture.
- "**Kage felt like there were other presences around.**" Nerezza recognizes him from this alley in S2 Ch6. And Anubis has a photo of this exact moment. So someone else was watching too.
- Frostbite: a glowing blue and black suit, a mask. "This one he recognized, but he couldn't quite put his finger on where. **His hand curled around nothing.**"
- Polar Shift card: frostbite "from the inside out," and "fire and explosions are the best way to take him down." Lucifer and Rebento are both in the tournament.
- "Horror…who is this?"
- "He's not random; I just met him yesterday." That's a great deadpan joke.
- The word "**pawn**" wakes Kage up. The motif comes back when Kelly calls the tournament kids "the pawns that put on a good show for you."
- "Sorry to ruin your 'smoldering aura farm.'"
- "**You sticky fucking freak**…I'll kill you!" This is Lucifer's exact phrase from S1 Ch7.
- "Sorry to give you the cold shoulder, Frost." A dad joke from the scariest man in the book.
- "I am not your teacher, or your savior. And I'm certainly no hero."
- The task: Khaled Nasser, Anubis.
- "There was something about Frostbite he really didn't like. He couldn't name it."
- Thanatos: "He is a pawn to us, but he can't know that." Frostbite: "The boss could always use extra info, but really, I just want to give him tasks that make him uncomfortable."
- "I don't care what he…" Frostbite cuts himself off. "**I don't care what the guy in the chair may do to me.**"

**What it's doing.** It expands the villain side into an organization with a boss, and plants Frostbite as the mystery inside the mystery.

**What works.** The power dynamics are clear and specific. Thanatos manipulates, Frostbite bullies, and Kage keeps insisting on a dignity nobody grants him. The final reveal gives the reader information Kage doesn't have, which creates tension for the next several chapters.

**What I'd push on.** Thanatos explains the pawn plan very openly to Frostbite right after Kage leaves. It works, but a villain this careful might say less. Letting Frostbite infer it while Thanatos smiles would keep him scarier.

**Score: 9/10.** The hand curling around nothing is the best quiet clue in the book.

## 1.14 Anubis-pheric Pressure

*1,366 words. Anubis's office, the next morning.*

**What happens.** Kage casts a glue key and breaks into Anubis's office. It's bare except for one Anubis action figure. He finds files labeled "Black Market," "Villain Encounters," and "Student Prospects," and photographs his class's pages, including notes on himself. The last photo is from last night: Kage pinned by Frostbite's ice. Anubis walks in. Kage lies that he came for advice. Anubis catches the lie about the collapse, gives a "systems" speech, and lets him go, then notices the open folder and that his door was unlocked.

**Details I caught.**

- The glue key cast is clever and in character.
- "The walls weren't painted." Huoyan later quotes Anubis: "I don't decorate my office because that would be a waste of money."
- **One Anubis action figure.** Kage wants a name like an action figure. Anubis owns one of himself.
- The notes:
  - Elias: "**Not showing signs of trauma.**" That's a huge seed, confirmed in S4 Ch3.
  - Kage asleep in class: "Why is he here? Should he be moved to general studies?"
  - Orion: "**Good fit for the Elite Program.**" That's the first mention of the Elite Program.
  - Kage at the spar: "Kage still needs a lot of work."
  - Robonoids: "**Self-destructive tendencies.**"
  - The race: "Exponential growth over a short period. Hero name stated to be: Sticky Situation."
  - Severine's barrier: "Advanced problem-solving skills. **Possible successor next to Lucifer.**"
  - Frostbite: "**How did he survive the attack?** Kage is possibly stronger than originally thought." That's chilling. It also suggests the camera saw the ice but not Thanatos stepping in.

- "We have systems this way for a reason."

**What I'd push on.** It's convenient that every relevant photo is in one folder on the desk. If that's intended, push it a hair further.

**Score: 9/10.** The chapter where Anubis stops being simple.

## 1.15 You're My Problem

# Character Dossiers: The Four Leads

## Kage Ozul ("Sticky Situation," "King," "Prince")

**At a glance.** Second-year hero student from Japan. Orphan, raised in foster homes. Arctic blue eyes (hidden for a while by gray contacts), scarlet hair that drooped "lazy" until he cut it into a spiky, cartoonish, action-figure shape in S2 Ch10. Eyeliner since then. About seven inches shorter than Orion. A gap in his smile since S3 Ch15. Wears Abby's gold whistle on a string around his neck. *Kage* is Japanese for "shadow," which is a sharp name for a boy who spent his first year being invisible, and who later escapes a shadow-user by flying toward the moon.

**Power: Adhesive Manipulation.** He consumes the lipids (fats) in his body and excretes adhesives through his nails, both fingernails and toenails. He's shown making double-sided tape, white glue, industrial tape, rubberized bouncy glue, cement and concrete, compressed explosive glue "mines," lipid-dense extra-strength tape, fire-stop caulk, tape confetti, and a glue key cast.

**Costs.** No food means no fuel, which is why he eats cheese, drank oil once, and empties minibars. Hardening draws cold from his own body, and overuse causes fever: 102°F, then 105°F, then 110°F, which should be fatal. Telekinesis nearly killed him the first time, and since then it seems to work only when he's certain of himself.

**What the book quietly says about his power.** It's more than glue. The evidence: he "refused to explain" it. Rhatella calls him a "human insulator." His hair stands on end when he uses telekinesis. His body "continued to shake and vibrate" after the first collapse. Huoyan says, "Something's off," and "I haven't told Anubis everything." Anubis's notes say "stronger than originally thought." Kage himself says, "maybe my DNA is meant for that." And he can do telekinesis even though his power behaves like a body-based "mutation," which Huoyan implies usually can't.

**The biomass.** Since S2 Ch7, part of Thanatos's body is inside him. It regenerates broken bone, burned skin, and torn muscle in seconds, but it can't regrow a part that's been removed, like his tooth. It flares with injury and a high heart rate. It costs huge amounts of energy. And Thanatos warned that it will "amplify your traits, especially the negative ones."

**Arc so far.**

1. **The ghost** (Prologue). Lazy by choice. "Staying small and safe."
2. **The penitent** (Section 1). Trains until he bleeds, picks fights, loses, and gets back up. Wins once, against Orion.
3. **The recruit** (S1 Ch12–14). Shakes a villain's hand because a villain was the first adult to say what he wanted to hear.
4. **The broken** (Section 2). Chooses Thanatos over the whistle. Watches Abby die. Is taken apart and rebuilt.
5. **The ghost again** (S2 Ch10–12). Blows a silent whistle for days. Gets a new face. Kidnaps the top student on a villain's errand.
6. **The performer** (Sections 3–4). Returns as a crowd favorite and wins three matches. Lies more easily each time. Gets crueler. And in the middle of it, sticks his tongue out at a scared kid.

**What makes him work.** Kage is funny, which is rarer and harder than people think. His jokes ("I almost didn't…stick…that landing," "H.O.T.," "Sticky Man") are bad on purpose, in character, and they show a boy who uses humor to manage pain. He's clever in a way the fights prove rather than state. And he's genuinely good underneath: the hero-name speech, carrying Orion to the nurse, twisting in midair to protect Orion's legs, putting the claymore back. The book is making the reader love him right as it starts making him worse. That's the right order.

**His core contradiction.** He wants to save people and he wants to be watched. The first instinct came from the store. The second came from being an orphan nobody ever cheered for. Anubis feeds the second and calls it heroism. Thanatos feeds the second and calls it evolution. Only Lance, and in her own way Abby, speak to the first.

**Key lines.**

- "Every time I save someone, I want to tell them my name and make them giggle."
- "People will call me King Kage one day. That's not arrogance, by the way. It's a spoiler."
- "Somebody paid for my invisibility with their life."
- "You can't break…what's already broken!"
- "I am really bad at saving people. Everyone I've tried to save, it never ended well for them."
- "Should a number one hero ever be number two?"

**Relationships.**

- **Orion**: rival, "not friends," the person he understands best in a fight. Kage named Orion's hero name. Neither knows the other's secret from the dome.
- **Abby**: the friend he didn't know he had until she died for him. Her whistle is his anchor. When it's off his neck, he loses control.
- **Ailani**: the person who sharpens him. He loves her. He keeps hurting her.
- **Lance**: his moral counterweight, and his newest real friend.
- **Thanatos**: the man who saved him, owns him, and lied to him.
- **Frostbite**: the man he bows to. His hand curls around nothing when Frostbite is near.
- **Anubis**: the man whose approval he wants and whose love he now knows is conditional.
- **Huoyan**: the teacher who actually worries about him, and whom he doesn't fully see yet.
- **Lucifer**: his mirror. Two boys with monsters inside them, both chasing Anubis.

**What I'd watch.** Two risks. First, the biomass could become an excuse. If every bad thing Kage does is blamed on the mass, he stops being responsible for anything. The book handles this well so far: the cruelty is his own, amplified. Keep it that way. Second, the "number one" hunger needs a cost that isn't physical. He's lost a tooth. He should eventually lose something he can't regenerate in a relationship.

**Prediction.** The book is heading toward a moment where Kage has to choose between being watched and saving someone, publicly, with the biomass pushing him toward the wrong answer. I think the whistle will be in his hand when it happens.

**Rating: 10/10 as a protagonist.** He's the reason the book works.

## Orion Vance ("Hermes," "Mercury," "Speed-Limiter's" rival)

**At a glance.** Tall, dark-skinned, dreadlocks, covered in gold (rings, loc rings, lip piercings, earrings, necklaces, bracelets). Amber-gold eyes. Seven inches taller than Kage, one inch taller than Lucifer. Enjoys slow walks as a "guilty pleasure." Says "I am Greek, Kage, not Roman," which could be a heritage hint or just a joke about the gods. His hero name, which he chose to keep, is Hermes, and Kage gave it to him.

**Power: Celestial Velocity.** Gold and violet comet light. Officially "up to two hundred miles per hour," broken in S2 Ch6 when he hit 300. Superhuman reflexes, wall-running, and "perception and timing." Techniques: Aether Step (a burst), Divine Relay (stored kinetic energy dumped into a kick), and Zephyr Mode (full output, painful to re-flare). With a passenger, about 100 mph. Costs: overheating, joint strain, and "a single misstep could tear him apart." His power also stretches time for him emotionally. When he tells Ailani that Kage is dead, "each millisecond began feeling like an eternity."

**Arc so far.** Orion starts as the calm, kind, competent one. He picks up the boy at the punching bag, befriends the lonely inventor, and says "perception and timing." Then the book breaks him in three steps:

1. **The dome** (S2 Ch7). Given the choice between his life and Kage's, he chooses his own at the count of three, and dresses it up as logic.
2. **The lie** (S2 Ch8 onward). He tells Ailani that Kage died. He doesn't tell anyone about Abby. He hides that Kage was working with Shifting Horror. And he admits to himself that "a part of him prayed for this."
3. **The anger** (S3 Ch5–6, S4 Ch5). Inazuma's racism and "heroes don't run from danger" crack him. He wins by a low blow, apologizes for it, resents apologizing, and drags Rebento's face across the floor.

Through all of it, he keeps Kage's whistle safe and raises it for him when no one else does.

**What makes him work.** He's the book's best example of a good person who did a bad thing and is now living inside it. Orion never becomes a villain. He becomes someone with a secret, which in this book is the more common kind of corruption. His "We're not friends" is a defense mechanism, and the book shows it in his body: the blush near Ailani, the punching bag "like a boxer aiming for someone's vitals," the smile "he desperately didn't want."

**Key lines.**

- "Speed isn't just running straight. It's perception and timing."
- "You improvise. I execute. That's our combo."
- "Let me out of here, and go after Kage… this choice is the most logical."
- "Why'd you have to go and die on me?! You piece of shit!"
- "I was taught never to speak ill of the dead."
- "Your perception of me is off… And, your timing is predictable."
- "I'm pro hero…Hermes."

**Relationships.**

- **Kage**: rival, "not friends," the person who brings out his competitiveness. He chose to let Kage be broken. Now he's protecting Kage's secret partly to protect his own.
- **Ailani**: his crush (the blush in S2 Ch6, S2 Ch13, S3 Ch12). He's comforting her over a boy who isn't dead, while falling for her.
- **Rhatella**: his first real friend in the book. He hasn't been shown with her since Section 1, which is a thread to pick back up.
- **Abby**: guilt. "He didn't take the time to get to know her."
- **Inazuma**: the person who found his wound and pressed on it.

**What I'd watch.** Orion has been off-screen as a point-of-view character since roughly S3 Ch6. His inner life is the second-most interesting in the book, and the reader hasn't heard his thoughts about the dome since Kage came back. He's owed a chapter where he's alone with what he did.

**Prediction.** Nerezza, Rebento, or Kage himself will expose the dome choice, and the confession will come at the worst possible time, probably right before or during a Kage–Orion grand final. And I think Orion will forgive himself before Kage forgives him.

**Rating: 9.5/10.** The book's quiet second protagonist.

## Abby Neosame

**At a glance.** Small, pale, blonde, with yellow eyes and a twin-layered halo. Wings she rarely shows (Kage never saw them until she died). Nearly silent steps. A dolphin-shaped backpack. Worked at a maid café. Finished her homework in the morning. Believes "God chose me to be the one who makes sure everybody's okay."

**Power: Eyes of Divinity.** She heals with yellow light and sees through people, including injuries, temperature, and things like "something zapping your energy from inside." The scanning **can't be turned off**, so she sees everyone's pain all the time. Limits: the patient has to be alive, it doesn't work on herself, and serious wounds need speed. Ultimate technique: **Divine Judgement**, an explosion of her own body that strikes enemies and herself.

**Arc.** Abby has the shortest arc and the biggest impact. She approaches Lucifer in her second week of school to help him, and gives him the distance he asks for. She meets Kage at the punching bag. Watching him rise again and again, she decides to be his "number one supporter." She gives him her dead brother's whistle. She's thrown out of Lucifer's doorway and goes to help Kage instead. She dies on the street, using her last breath to tell Kage that everything is going to be alright.

**Her wound.** Her brother was "incredibly sick." She was "too young for doctors to trust her." She was told to wait, she waited, and he died. "She never tried, and that's what's been killing her." That's why she won't wait for Lucifer, and why she walks the city all night looking for two boys who didn't ask for her.

**What makes her work.** Abby is goodness without being naive. She reads Lucifer correctly, respects his boundary, and is sad without being pathetic. And her death is designed around her own rules: she can see every wound, she freezes at the sight of pain, and she can't heal herself. Her power kills her in a way only her power could.

**Key lines.**

- "It's okay, Kage. Everything is going to be alright."
- "Nurses and doctors have patient confidentiality!"
- "Is helping others not a desire?"
- "I'm happy to not be your friend, Lucifer."
- "Kage…I will be your number one supporter…"
- "I will always be your number one supporter, Kage."

**Relationships.**

- **Kage**: the person she chose to support, possibly with a crush (Huoyan suspects one; Ailani asks). He felt it too late.
- **Lucifer**: her patient. He cares more than he admits. His tail stayed stiff when he asked if she was with them.
- **Orion**: a friend she brought water to. He's guilty he never got to know her.
- **Her brother**: her ghost, and the maker of the whistle.

**Is she dead?** The book says so through Orion, Kage, and the narration. But there's no body ("there was just…nothing to find"). She "burst completely into light." The narration says "not every ability of Abby is known" and that some think she "may actually have fallen from heaven." And the section named after her continues for nine chapters after she's gone. I discuss this in Theories. My honest view: whether or not she returns, her death has to stay real in its consequences, or it will undercut the best chapter in the book.

**What I'd push on.** Her brother needs one concrete detail: a name, a laugh, what his enhancement was. Right now he's a reason. Give him a face, and the whistle gets heavier.

**Rating: 9.5/10.** She's in fewer chapters than anyone in the main cast, and she's the one the book is built around.

## Ailani Shoma

**At a glance.** Latina, Black, and Japanese. Curly hair, often in a bun. Tanned skin. Pink workout clothes. A purple, fluorescent dorm room with string lights. Swears in Spanish when furious ("HIJO DE PUTA," "PENDEJO," "IMBÉCIL," "CRETINO," "CÁLLATE"). Has friends in general studies, and is friends with Geneki. Dances alone in the mirror room. Wants to be a top **choreographer and dancer**, and joined the hero course to be "dependable" for her family and to fund that dream.

**Power: Crystal Release.** A chromatic sigil appears on her hands. The sigil turns ninety degrees for telekinesis. She makes, shapes, and controls crystals from nothing: projectiles, walls, weapons up to a war hammer, and delicate things like a butterfly whose wings flap. Range: 25 meters. Large constructs last 10 to 15 seconds. Small ones last almost indefinitely. Cost: overuse locks her hands in crystal from the fingertips to the wrists.

**Arc so far.** Ailani has spent the book reacting to other people's drama, and the book knows it. She keeps getting cut off ("This isn't about you right now"). She keeps holding her tongue because Kage or Orion asks her to. She's "tired of being reactionary." She's had no real fight: her first-round opponent fell over on purpose. Her section opens with her past and ends with her walking out to perform. It's built like an entrance.

**The romance.** She dated Kage in first year. It started with a crystal to the gut and honey toast, and a napkin with a heart. She broke up with him because he did nothing and flirted with everyone. She took him back for one weekend, then dumped him again over Reina. She mourned him and hit him when he came back. "Keep my number." She asks if he and Abby were ever together, which is jealousy. And she's been spending every day with Orion, holding his hand and rooming with him, not noticing he blushes.

**What makes her work.** She's funny, perceptive, and physically confident. She reads Orion's control precisely ("That speed is like a light jog for Orion"). She calls out Inazuma's racism when nobody else does. And she's the only person who can calm Kage with a touch. The book also gives her a life outside heroism (dance, family, general-studies friends), which makes her the most grounded person in the cast.

**Key lines.**

- "Dull tools only get duller without someone like me to sharpen them!"
- "We aren't back together yet."
- "And, Kage, lose my number." / "And Kage? Keep my number."
- "I think he's…trying to prove he's better. Not the better fighter, better in every category."
- "EVERYONE CÁLLATE!"
- "This is my time, and I've been dying to perform."

**What I'd watch.** She has been the book's best supporting character, and her own section has to make her a lead. That means a fight that shows what her power can do, a choice that's about her and not about Kage, and a moment where her dancing matters. A crystal technique that looks like choreography would be perfect.

**Prediction.** She beats Lance with a fight that's half dance. Then she faces Orion, and the fight forces the love triangle into the open. If she reaches the grand final against Kage, it pays off "I don't want to fight you" and "if we fought, I'd kick your ass." I'd bet on that collision somewhere in Section 4.

**Rating: 9/10.** Waiting in the wings, and ready.


# Character Dossiers: The Class

## Lucifer Tulivalo ("Lu," "Luci")

**At a glance.** Tall (an inch shorter than Orion, which annoys him), orange hair, light blue eyes, dark red horns, and a tail that betrays his feelings: it wags when he's happy, goes stiff when he's worried. Pale skin. A dorm with one bedroom, medals, a trophy case, and a black claymore engraved "Minkowski." He came first in the entrance exam. He's "the top second-year hero student in the world," which is literally true since there's only one program. *Tuli* and *valo* are Finnish for "fire" and "light," so his name is "light-bringer firelight." That's a lot of light for a boy carrying a demon.

**Power: Hellspawn.** He can produce fire from any part of his body (fists, horns, tail, eyes, mouth) at any temperature, "from a surgical burn" to steel-melting. And inside him is **Abaddon**.

**The secret.** He never fully sleeps, because sleep lets Abaddon out. That's why he's always tired and why Abby saw something draining him. He's cruel on purpose: "I turn up my poor traits to get people to not want to talk to me." He keeps three people close: Elias, who knows and watches him; Reina, whose power can absorb his heat; and, from a distance, Abby, whom he pushed away to keep her safe.

**Arc so far.** He's the school's bully and the book's most sympathetic villain-adjacent student. He threatens to burn Kage and Abby alive. He tells Ailani about Reina. He makes a deal with Anubis to end Kage's rise. He breaks Reina's gear and ignores her after winning. He burns Kage's face assuming the nurses can fix it. Then, inside his own head, he finally accepts the demon as part of himself, mouths "I'm sorry" to Elias, and lets Abaddon out on live television. He loses anyway. Afterward he confesses the deal. And he votes to keep Kage in the school.

**Key lines.**

- "Listen to me, you sticky fucking freak."
- "I know nothing is free. What's your angle?"
- "At the end of the day, angels can't befriend demons."
- "I don't want her hanging around and ending up hurt!"
- "Only Min gets to call me that."
- "I exposed myself in front of millions of people and you couldn't just lose?!"

**What makes him work.** The book gives him a reason for every cruelty, and it doesn't use the reason to excuse him. He hurts Reina. He hurts Abby. He hurts Kage. And he's also a kid who hasn't slept properly in years. The tail is a brilliant device: an honest body part on a dishonest person.

**What I'd watch.** His motive for telling Ailani about Reina is the one cruelty without a stated reason. And now that the world has seen Abaddon, Lucifer's future (his place in the program, his relationship with Anubis, how the public treats him) is the biggest open question in the student cast.

**Prediction.** Lucifer ends up with Huoyan, his fire-power counterpart, as Anubis's "you'll officially be Huoyan's problem" line suggests. Huoyan is the one adult equipped to teach him to live with a fire that wants out. And I think Lucifer and Kage end up as reluctant allies, two monster-carriers who understand each other.

**Rating: 9/10.**

## Abaddon

**At a glance.** Ten feet tall. Black skin, scorched bone, a tail "thick as a tree trunk," blood-red eyes, taloned feet, horns tipped with fire. Speaks about himself in the third person, in capital letters, when he's free. In Lucifer's mind he sits in a child's chair at the kitchen table of Lucifer's childhood home, and speaks in the first person. His name is the Hebrew word for "destruction" and the angel of the abyss in Revelation.

**Power.** Everything Lucifer has, plus mass, strength, skin too thick for concrete fists, and enough heat to vaporize glue. Weaknesses: he needs air, and he can be knocked out.

**What he wants.** To be out. "I am tired of living my life watching yours through this domestic kitchen window!" He argues he and Lucifer "are one" and that Lucifer's exhaustion comes from Abaddon trying to escape. He's been out before, "after years," which means some earlier escape.

**Key lines.**

- "FREEDOM IS SWEETER THAN ABADDON COULD HAVE EVER IMAGINED!"
- "You wear those devil horns, but you won't accept your true self?"
- "You're a secondary player in Kage Ozul's story."
- "Your selective intelligence confounds me. So smart, and so stupid."
- "The truth is so terrible that you need to make all of this look like your grand plan?! OH, I LIKE YOU!"

**What makes him work.** He's funny, articulate, and right about people. He reads Kage's self-deception in one line. He's what Kage could become if the biomass wins.

**Rating: 9/10.**

## Elias Minkowski ("Min")

**At a glance.** Short (about an inch taller than Reina), jet-black hair, glasses (Rhatella-made, broken by Kage, not yet replaced), a boring outfit. Lucifer's best friend and rival for the top of the class. "The quietest student in the sophomore class." He seems to know when people are lying. He sleeps over in Lucifer's room. He insists the claymore with his family name on it hangs perfectly straight. *Minkowski* is the name of the mathematician behind "Minkowski spacetime," and Elias's power stops things in spacetime. That's a great name match.

**Power: Chronal Inertia.** Anything he touches stops "dead in spacetime." Forces acting on it are banked and released all at once when he lets go. Each hold has a three-minute clock that burns only when the world pushes back; living things can struggle against it. Each extra hold is another live calculation in his head, which has given him "superhuman perception and razor sharp processing." Vital functions continue under a hold, which is how Kage beat him.

**Arc so far.** He's cold, analytical, and dismissive of Kage, whom he humiliates on camera ("for food instead of the goodness in his heart"). He loses. He refuses Kage's friendship in the most precise breakdown of Kage's secret in the book: "either… illogical, or… hiding something. The worst part is, I believe the answer is both." He apologizes to his friends for losing. When Abaddon appears, he runs to the bathroom shaking. He punches Lucifer for summoning the demon, deliberately unfrozen so Lucifer feels every hit. He hugs Kage. And he says: "You know I never wanted to feel that feeling again."

**The history.** Lucifer: "Me and Elias have history." Anubis's file: "Not showing signs of trauma." Abaddon: "Being out again after years." The Hellspawn card: "Even fewer have lived after seeing it." Elias: "I was worried that the demon might get out and I wouldn't be there to stop it!" Something happened when they were children, Abaddon got out, and Elias was there.

**Key lines.**

- "If I said it's crooked, then it's crooked, Luci."
- "You are strong. That's very admirable."
- "Most people look at a car crash as they drive by, but what do they do after? They drive off."
- "I cannot be partners with someone I cannot trust."
- "We hate him and don't trust him, but we're not gonna kick him out with some stupid popularity contest."

**What makes him work.** He looks like a cold genius and turns out to be a frightened, loyal kid who has made himself his best friend's jailer. Every cold thing about him reads differently once you know that.

**What I'd watch.** He's the one person who could figure out Kage's secret on his own. He's already halfway there. Use that.

**Prediction.** The claymore belonged to someone in Elias's family who was hurt or killed in the earlier Abaddon escape. Lucifer keeps it on his wall as penance or as a promise. And Elias's power is the one thing that can hold Abaddon still.

**Rating: 9.5/10.** The best-designed supporting character.

## Reina Cruz ("Cruz")

**At a glance.** Small, white hair, sparkling dark blue eyes, strong posture, thin arms. Lucifer's girlfriend. Elias's friend. Says "BOOM!" with her whole body. *Reina Cruz* is Spanish for "queen" and "cross," so the devil is dating the cross.

**Power: Heat Charge.** She absorbs heat and converts it into muscle mass. The more she absorbs, the bigger and stronger she gets, and the less mobile. Punching burns it off, and she shrinks. Empty, she's dizzy and immobile. She uses a Rhatella-made pack on her back to store and release heat on command (shrink to dodge, regrow to grab). "The only one in the world that can truly say they can handle Lucifer's heat."

**Arc so far.** She's the cheerful one in Lucifer's group, and the one nobody tells the truth to. She wins her first match without understanding how. She absorbs her boyfriend's flames and throws him around in the funniest fight in the tournament. He breaks her gear and ignores her after winning. She learns about Abaddon from the TV, along with the rest of the world. She sits with Kage's group "as long as it takes till they apologize."

**Key lines.**

- "I'll get you like BOOM!!!!"
- "That…could've been me…"
- "Whether we get in the Elite Program or not, we'll still have each other!"
- "Awww, babe you shouldn't have!"
- "So, both of you knew about this and kept it from me?!"

**What makes her work.** Her power is the perfect counter to her boyfriend's, which explains why he keeps her close. And the book lets her be hurt in a specific way: not by the demon, but by being kept out.

**What I'd watch.** She's at a turning point. She could break up with Lucifer, or demand to be let into the secret. Either way, she shouldn't go back to being the cheerful one without consequence.

**Rating: 8.5/10.**

## Rhatella Gardner (with C.O.R.E.)

**At a glance.** Four foot eight, brown skin, a cleft lip, metallic wires growing from her head, swirling goggles, rat-like ears. A cyan bodysuit and a lab coat. Hisses when startled. Reads romance novels. Ships everyone. Stamps every invention with a cartoon of her own face. Lives in a dorm that's bigger on the inside, "five electrical factories mashed into one." Daughter of Gene Gardner. Sister of Geneki.

**Power: Wireplay.** She grows and controls cables like limbs, takes over circuits, runs electricity harmlessly across her skin, senses disruptions through any wired device, and "might one day manipulate solid metal."

**C.O.R.E.** (Centralized Operational Response Engine) runs the dorm. Its objectives are to process stimuli and respond, protect its creator, and protect itself and the facility. It took Kage's blood. It sighs. It once kicked Kage down a shaft with a giant boot.

**Her gear in the tournament.** Elias's glasses (broken by Kage), Rebento's gumball machines, Lance's hilt, and Reina's heat pack (broken by Lucifer). She builds alphabetically and is stuck on "O" for Ozul because of his "human insulator" body.

**Arc so far.** She's lonely. "As long as I'm useful, right?" Orion becomes her first real friend. Kage becomes another. She watches her inventions get broken on live TV, cries, ships the boys again, and admits: "I wish I was the one they were all fighting over… Lord knows he wouldn't take my heart!"

**What makes her work.** She's the book's comic relief with real sadness built in. Kage's description of her to Lance is the best summary: "The only thing she loves doing is creating gadgets for people because that's the only way she feels close to them."

**What I'd watch.** She's been offstage since Section 1 apart from the interlude. She has Kage's blood from before the biomass. She's the one person who could compare it to his blood now. That's a loaded gun on her lab bench.

**Prediction.** Rhatella finishes Kage's gear, and it's built to handle something she doesn't understand yet, the biomass. Or her analysis of his old blood sample reveals something about his power's real nature.

**Rating: 8.5/10.**

## Severine Neant

**At a glance.** Five foot eleven, bluish-violet hair, purple clothes and eyes. Speaks formally and in lists of "dislikes": disruptions, incompetence, foolishness. Laughs until she cries. Drinks tea. Hasn't spoken to a classmate in a year and a half, except Kage. *Néant* is French for "nothingness," which fits someone whose power pushes everything away.

**Power: Repulse-Redux.** Repulsive force fields: barriers, redirection, flight, shockwaves, launched objects, focused blasts. Costs: stamina, weakening with prolonged use, concentration for complex fields, and recoil against powerful attacks.

**Arc so far.** She beats Kage cleanly, steps on his chest, and tells him to come back when he's "significantly superior." She loses in Round 1 to Lucifer's push without a scratch on her, has a public meltdown, and screams for the only classmate she ever talked to. He turns away. She watches the rest at home and decides their rematch is "inevitable."

**What makes her work.** She's a perfectionist whose perfection isolates her completely, and the meltdown shows it. Her calling Kage's name in front of everyone is the saddest moment in the first round.

**What I'd watch.** The meltdown is very long and very loud. The reader should never feel the book is laughing at her. Her return, presumably the rematch, should give her dignity.

**Prediction.** Severine gets into the Elite Program despite losing in Round 1, because Gene or Anubis bends the rule, and it causes resentment. Or she doesn't, and the rematch with Kage happens outside the system.

**Rating: 8/10.**

## Zulfiya Shifo

**At a glance.** A sophomore classmate with blue (or light blue) hair that she moves "like fingers." Calls Huoyan "Xiansheng," a Chinese honorific. Calls Kage "King," "your highness," "your majesty," "my sovereign," and now "Prince." Hates egos, except Kage's. *Zulfiya* is a common Uzbek name, and *shifo* means "healing" or "cure" in Uzbek.

**Power: Mending Mane.** Healing hair that wraps people and restores wounds and stamina. It also reads bodies precisely: temperature to the degree, unconsciousness to the minute. The hair can be ripped, and burned or torn hair grows back slowly.

**Arc so far.** She heals Kage twice. She makes the King nickname stick. She forfeits her shot at the Elite Program to "support" him, which detonates his trauma. She's the one who tells him he can't regrow a lost part. She keeps noticing the whistle.

**What makes her work.** She's a second healer in a book that just lost its first. Kage's reaction to her "support" shows how badly Abby's death broke him.

**What I'd watch.** Her forfeit cost her the Elite Program. That's a real sacrifice, and it deserves to come back. Does she regret it? Does Kage feel responsible?

**Rating: 8/10.**

## Cheyenne Bandalore ("Partner," "Che")

**At a glance.** Unbuttoned blazer, white blouse, a loosened red necktie, a crimson-and-gold serape, spurred boots, and a cowboy hat "she never took off." Shadowed eyes with "the weary intensity of a desert hawk." A strawberry lollipop held like a cigar. A metallic jingle, "either keys or pocket change." Southern slang: "partner," "bless your heart," "two shakes of a lamb's tail," "I reckon." Lance's girlfriend.

**Power: unknown.** Her power card is literally cut off mid-sentence when Elias freezes her. She put her hands in her pockets to prepare her attack. She thinks her power probably can't do telekinesis.

**Arc so far.** She's the one who noticed Kage's tears during the King speech and decided to believe him. She tells him to "take the time to enjoy being a prince." She loses to Elias in seconds and hides how bad she feels about it. She lends Kage her hat, by force, and is the stake in his bet with Lance.

**What makes her work.** She's a cowboy in a superhero school, and the book plays it straight enough that she becomes a person rather than a costume. The hat is a great prop.

**Prediction.** Her power has something to do with what's in her pockets: coins, or something she throws or loads, like a gunslinger. The jingle is the clue. Please don't explain it until it's dramatic.

**Rating: 8/10.**

## Lance Faisceau ("Fai," "Lancey," "Sir Lance")

**At a glance.** Gray hair that reflects light. Stern face. Says he's class representative; Rebento says Lance is vice-rep. Dates Cheyenne. Has money ("rich boy"). Calls everyone by their last name until he likes them. *Faisceau* is French for "beam," which fits his beam swords, and the "Lance" and "Avalon" pairing makes him a Lancelot figure.

**Power: Avalon's Legacy.** Beam swords of yellow energy in any shape (rapier, greatsword, arming sword). They cut through rigid things effortlessly, but flesh moves with the blade instead of being cut, so a hit lands as a searing strike. His Rhatella-made hilt protects his hands. "There seems to be a limit to Lance, a point where he chooses that he needs to stop even when his hilt and hands are fine, but people aren't quite sure why!"

**Backstory.** His father was a high-earning villain in Quebec who taught him to steal, run heists, and break in, and who spent time with him only then. The father was arrested. Lance defended him, until he watched a frog approach some ants on their way home and turned the frog toward the pond without thinking. He realized he'd hated every heist. "I try to hold people to the standard I didn't hold to myself."

**Arc so far.** He helps Kage beat Lucifer to the door: "Heroes help other heroes." He objects to "win by any means." He knights Volkan. He refuses Kage's apology until he sees honorable fighting, then becomes Kage's closest new friend after one night of talking. He casts the first vote to keep Kage.

**Key lines.**

- "Heroes help other heroes."
- "Fight them how you'd want to be fought."
- "Oh, the straight man doesn't get to be funny too? You're putting me in slots, Kage."
- "Okay, Anubis."
- "I trust Kage deeply."
- "When fighting sometimes you end up as someone else."

**What makes him work.** He's the moral center of Section 3 and the clearest mirror for Kage: a villain's son choosing heroism, beside a hero choosing villains in secret.

**What I'd watch.** His "limit" and "you end up as someone else" are clearly connected, and probably connected to his father. When he fights Ailani, the book will likely show it.

**Prediction.** Lance's limit is a fear of becoming his father, the point in a fight where winning would require something "dishonorable" that he knows how to do. The Ailani match will push him toward it. Separately, I'd watch for his father to turn out to be connected to the villain organization, because a "high earning villain" and "the boss" live in the same world.

**Rating: 9.5/10.** One night in a hotel room made him one of the best characters in the book.

## Rebento Azucre ("Sugar Bomb")

**At a glance.** Brown hair. Says he's the main class representative. Pops gumballs. Single, and "his relationships don't last longer than a week." He introduced Cheyenne and Lance. Crude ("Were her lips cold during kissing too?"). Protective of his friends. *Azucre* is Galician for "sugar," and *rebentar* is Portuguese for "to burst," so his name is close to "sugar burst."

**Power: Sweet-Toothed Surprise.** He turns the glucose in non-living things into kinetic charge, so candy becomes bombs. Gumball machines on his belt (Rhatella-made), a sprinkle shaker, jawbreakers, hard candies, and a hidden cherry gumball in a knuckle-cracking fist. Telekinesis, signaled by rainbow swirl eyes. He can't charge living things, and charging costs him energy. Explosions smell like burning caramel.

**Arc so far.** He helps throw Kage over the crowd. He blows Alex out of the ring. He defends Cheyenne from Orion. He's the only one who says out loud that Kage's sudden healing makes no sense and calls a vote to remove him, which fails unanimously. He loses to Orion, accuses Kage of coaching, and moves to sit with Lucifer and Elias.

**What makes him work.** He's right about Kage, and nobody believes him. That makes him the book's Cassandra, and that's a good role to give a crude, funny character.

**What I'd watch.** As noted, the book is at risk of making Rebento unlikeable for being correct. Give him a scene where his suspicion comes from fear for his friends, not ego.

**Prediction.** Rebento finds real evidence of Kage's villain connection, maybe through a sighting or a detail from the night of the kidnapping, and has to decide whether to use it.

**Rating: 8/10.**

## Inazuma Sokudo

**At a glance.** Blue hair, dark blue eyes ("the red to my blue"). Loud: "WAZZZUPPPPPPPP!!!" Knew Kage in middle school in Japan. Texted Kage all through first year, including the maid-outfit photo. Calls Orion "Big Vance" and a "big black dolt." *Inazuma* is Japanese for "lightning," and *sokudo* means "speed."

**Power: Lightning Fast.** Super-speed in blue light. Friction stores static electricity in his body, which he releases as lightning blasts. If he doesn't discharge, he burns out. A misstep can send him flying the wrong way.

**Arc so far.** He's the "another speedster" from Kage's S1 Ch2 complaint, and the maid-photo sender from S1 Ch5. He makes racist comments to Orion, refuses to apologize, uses them as a weapon in the match, and loses to a low blow. He's out of the Elite Program.

**What makes him work.** He's a real kind of person, the friend from home who knew you before and won't let you change. Ailani's "He kind of reminds me of you" is the most uncomfortable true thing anyone says to Kage.

**What I'd watch.** He made racist comments and faced only a low blow. Orion apologized; Inazuma didn't. If the book wants that injustice to stand, fine. But someone, ideally Kage, should eventually say clearly that Inazuma was wrong, because Kage's defense of him is one of Kage's real failures.

**Rating: 7.5/10.** He's effective, and unpleasant on purpose.

## Volkan Salyangozu ("Knight Volkan")

**At a glance.** Big, with dark red eyes, always in a black hoodie. Shy. Late to class in Section 1, punished with fifty laps, and forgotten by Kage for two sections. *Volkan* is Turkish for "volcano," and *salyangoz* is Turkish for "snail."

**Power: Volcano Snail.** He's built like a scaly-foot gastropod, a real deep-sea snail with an iron-plated shell. He has a massive iron shell on his back, tucks and rolls, tolerates extreme heat, and has gills. "He may be slow to move, but he tries to think fast!"

**Arc so far.** He loses to Lance by choice, after Lance stops him from rolling out, offers him a free win, and he refuses ("I want to earn it! I'm not just the slow kid!"). He's knighted in front of the crowd. He comes to wish Kage luck, which moves Kage more than anything else.

**What makes him work.** He's a running joke turned into one of the tournament's most moving moments.

**Prediction.** His heat resistance makes him a natural match for Lucifer or Abaddon. Watch for him to matter in a fire-related crisis.

**Rating: 8/10.**

## Geneki Gardner ("Gen")

**At a glance.** About eight feet tall, extremely muscular, a lazy eye, rat ears like Rhatella's, and a tail that's a cut-off stump. "Doesn't show emotion much." Ailani's friend: "She's a very nice girl." Gene's daughter.

**Power.** Strength enhanced by green energy. A stomp that "nearly disintegrated" the concrete floor.

**Arc so far.** She loses Round 1 by breaking the floor so hard she's technically underneath it. Her mother corrects her on live TV. She asks to go home, "Alone, preferably."

**What makes her work.** She's barely on the page and still one of the saddest characters. The stump tail, the public correction, and "alone, preferably" all point to a story about her mother.

**Prediction.** The tail is a story, possibly an injury her mother's ambitions caused, or a surgery. Geneki is one of the strongest people in the class and one of the least interested in being seen. She could be important later.

**Rating: 7.5/10.** Small role, big potential.

## Youseff Nasser

**At a glance.** About 5'3", brown-skinned. Twiddles his thumbs and picks his nose in front of a crowd chanting his family name. Anubis's younger brother. Doesn't want to be a hero.

**Power: unknown.** His power is never shown.

**Arc so far.** At the bell, he shouts "OH NO! I've tripped and fallen out of bounds!" and flops onto the grass. He tells Ailani the Elite Program "sounds like torture," lets her crystal hit him, and laughs at his brother. Huoyan smirks. Elias leaves him off the list of classmates he studied.

**Rating: 7.5/10.**

## Alexander Lund

**At a glance.** Green hair, flower earrings. Anxious. His mother cheers for him from the front row. *Lund* means "grove" in Scandinavian languages, which with the flowers and green hair suggests a plant power.

In the Abaddon fight, he pushes his mother's hand away so he can keep watching. When Kage sticks his tongue out at him, he laughs and starts the chant that turns the crowd.

# Character Dossiers: The Adults

## Huoyan Niao ("Hellfeather," "Mr. Niao," "Xiansheng Huoyan," "Hon")

**At a glance.** Tall, pale, bright white hair, usually-hidden wings. A strict homeroom teacher who "loathe[s] all of my students equally." English is his second language (Anubis corrects his grammar). He "barely drink[s] anymore." He sleeps at the school ("I sleep here"). He runs his own agency. He used to be Anubis's sidekick, and very likely more than that. *Huǒyàn niǎo* is Mandarin for "flame bird," which is to say, phoenix.

**Power: Phoenix.** A "walking flame made flesh." He radiates and fires heat. Flaming wing feathers can be shot as projectiles or used as a shield. He has a body alteration (the wings) and a controllable enhancement: "two in one." Weakness: water on his wings puts the flames out for a while.

**Arc so far.** Every time the book shows Huoyan's surface, it shows what's underneath a page later. He wants to break up the first spar, then lets it go. He's the teacher who forces fifty laps, and the one who panics when Kage collapses. He threatens and slams heads, and he admits he dreamed Kage and Orion "could go so far together." He's the only adult searching for Abby, refreshing his phone in the middle of the tournament. He's the only adult who tries to stop the Abaddon fight. He's right about Kage's math and wrong about Kage's chances. And he's the one who says "I haven't told Anubis everything."

**Key lines.**

- "I have morons for students."
- "I loathe all of my students equally, and I haven't told Anubis everything."
- "My students are good children. They may be dysfunctional, but all of them are truly heroic."
- "One times one is less than zero point five times three."
- "I teach all heroes, not just ones who punch well… Pacifists make great rescue heroes."
- "I sleep here."
- "I am mildly contented by your return, Ozul."

**What makes him work.** He's the adult the book trusts. Gruff people who secretly care are common, but Huoyan is specific: an ex-sidekick who stayed when his partner became a brand, living at the school, teaching kids who mostly can't stand him.

**What I'd watch.** "I haven't told Anubis everything" is the book's most explicit promise from an adult, and it's unpaid. Huoyan knows something about Kage that Anubis doesn't.

**Prediction.** Huoyan knows something about Kage's power, or his history, that explains why a failing first-year student was kept in the top class. He'll reveal it at the moment it can hurt or help the most. And when Lucifer is cut off by Anubis, Huoyan is the one who takes him in.

**Rating: 9.5/10.** The best adult in the book.

## Khaled Nasser ("Anubis," "Number One," "Mr. Know-it-all")

**At a glance.** Tall, gray-haired, always in sunglasses. The number-one hero in the world. Head of the hero program. A bare, unpainted office with one decoration: an Anubis action figure. Says "Sigh" out loud. Calls Huoyan "Hon" while insisting they're "simply coworkers." Has a younger brother, Youseff. Named after the Egyptian god who guides the dead and weighs their hearts against a feather. Note that his old partner is named *Hellfeather*.

**Power: unknown.** "His own enhancement seemed to activate" in S1 Ch2. It's never named.

**What he does.** He's the program's salesman. He wants cameras in the dorms ("despite my asking"). He keeps photo files on students without their knowledge, with notes on their trauma and potential. He asked to put the tournament on TV. He celebrates merch sales and record viewership, and says "That's not important right now!" about a missing student. He accepts a deal to drop Kage if Lucifer gives him a good fight. He watches Kage break his own bones and holds Huoyan back: "Isn't that what being a hero is all about?" He saw Kage on camera the night of the kidnapping and told no one. He knew about Abaddon. And he wants Kage as his successor "someday soon," because "an orphaned failure turned number one hero just sells itself."

**And yet.** He's also the first adult to call Kage "brilliant." He talked Huoyan out of punishing him twice. He's delighted, genuinely, when Kage challenges his authority. "You don't put on a heroic act; you just act heroic" is the kindest thing anyone says about Kage.

**Key lines.**

- "You two were brilliant."
- "We have systems this way for a reason."
- "Maybe both of them just needed mental health days."
- "Are you challenging my authority as head of the hero program?… That's wonderful!"
- "An orphaned failure turned number one hero just sells itself!"
- "As someone who needs rescuing, I wouldn't care if you needed to shoot the villain."
- "THAT IS HOW YOU PUT ON A SHOW!"

**What makes him work.** He's the book's thesis in a person. He isn't evil. He's a man who has confused being seen with being good, and he has the power to teach that to children.

**What I'd watch.** He's very close to being a straight antagonist. He'll be stronger if he keeps the warmth too, and if the reader can never be sure whether he's playing a long game. "I was worried you might've been compromised" suggests he suspects Kage's villain ties. Is he letting it happen? Using it?

**Prediction.** Anubis knows more about Kage's involvement with villains than he admits, and is cultivating it, possibly to use Kage as a lead to the boss. The "someday soon" also suggests he has a reason to step down: an illness, an injury, or a plan. And the Security Accords, which would put cameras everywhere, have his fingerprints on them.

**Rating: 9/10.**

## Gene Gardner

**At a glance.** Tall and rat-like, with large ears, a snout, and whiskers. A green suit, green glasses, and black hair. Creator of the Elite Tournament and the Elite Program. Loves announcing. Mother of Rhatella and Geneki.

**Arc so far.** She's the tournament's chaos agent. She makes the rules ("as stated in the rules, the ones I just made up"), changes the match order to undermine Anubis's deal, and asks the crowd whether to continue. She also corrects her daughter in public ("I thought I taught you better") and tells Zulfiya to "go home if you want! I don't care, as long as you move fast!"

**Key lines.**

- "Did you forget this is MY tournament?"
- "I'm not going to gamble away a kid's future to try and save a possibly burning ship."
- "Sticking it to Anubis is fun."
- "It's not what I think. It's what they think."

**What makes her work.** She's both the person who saves Kage from the deal and a parent whose daughter wants to be "alone, preferably." The book lets her be both.

**What I'd watch.** Her reason for switching the order doesn't quite hold. Huoyan points out she's gambling with Kage's future too, and she doesn't answer. That's fine if it's deliberate, and I suspect it is: she's not as principled as she says, she just likes beating Anubis.

**Prediction.** Gene decides who gets into the Elite Program, and her choices will be political. I'd also watch Geneki's tail.

**Rating: 8/10.**

## Kelly Iasacht

**At a glance.** Blond, blue-eyed. A consistent top hero who wants Anubis's spot. He gets less government money because he chose not to teach at the school. He has a wife and a son. The son "didn't end up showing up." He carries an "Irish remedy" in a vial. *Iasacht* is an Irish word (I believe it means something like "loan" or "foreign").

**Arc so far.** He's sent to escort Severine because he isn't staff, so the program "isn't fully liable." He mocks Anubis. He chugs the beer Anubis offered Huoyan. He takes Huoyan's phone and tells him to rest. He wakes the boys with smelling salts. He calls the remaining contestants "the pawns that put on a good show for you."

**What makes him work.** He's the outsider pro who can say what Huoyan can't, and his small kindness to Huoyan is a nice surprise.

**Prediction.** His son is a classmate, probably the twentieth student we haven't met, and the reason he "didn't show" will matter.

**Rating: 7.5/10.**

# Character Dossiers: The Villains

## Thanatos Shioya ("Shifting Horror," "Horror," "Thany")

**At a glance.** Six feet tall. A large scar from face to neck, which he rubs with his thumb. Purple eyes. Black hair fading to white at the tips. A black suit and shirt, with a white tie. "One of the most notorious villains currently on the underworld scene." Thanatos is the Greek personification of death, which makes him a counterpart to Anubis.

**Power: Bio-Forge.** He controls his own cells: density, shape, and new appendages. Anything he touches, organic or not, can be absorbed. He regenerates, and physical attacks barely hurt him. Costs: too many transformations in a row make his body fail. Some materials "reject integration." Losing focus brings migraines, then blackouts. Absorbed things leave "instincts" that scratch at his mind. And giving away biomass makes him smaller, weaker, and "starving."

**What he does.** He finds Kage at his lowest, tells him the truth about his self-destruction, and recruits him with "a few favors." He calls Kage a partner in public and a pawn in private. He takes an ice spike for Kage, then says he won't save him again. He saves him again. He watches Kage's limbs get broken and referees the game. He rebuilds Kage from his own body without consent, and leaves for Russia. When he sees Kage on TV, he wants to kill Frostbite.

**Key lines.**

- "Animals that never stop running don't evolve. They die."
- "Do you want to run past me or run with me?"
- "I am not your teacher, or your savior. And I'm certainly no hero."
- "He is a pawn to us, but he can't know that."
- "Most predators tear their meal to shreds the moment they catch it… You? You deliver it untouched."
- "This mass inside you will amplify your traits, especially the negative ones."

**What makes him work.** He's right. Everything he tells Kage about Kage is true, and that's why it works. And the rebuild shows him paying a real physical cost for his "project," which keeps him from being simple.

**What I'd watch.** He's been offstage since S2 Ch10, apart from Chapter NULL. When he comes back, he's angry at Frostbite and has a "serious talk" pending with Nerezza. His return should change Kage's situation immediately.

**Prediction.** Thanatos knows more about Kage than he lets on ("A boy who's gone through what he has"). Either he was involved with the convenience store, or he's been watching Kage for the boss for longer than one night in an alley.

**Rating: 9.5/10.**

## Frostbite

**At a glance.** A glowing blue-and-black suit with a mask and compartments (clothes, makeup, contacts). Under it, pale skin with dark, blistered fingertips, "like the cold was eating him from the inside out." He calls Thanatos "Horror" and "Thany." Kage bows to him.

**Power: Polar Shift.** Total creation and control of ice. Cost: frostbite that spreads from the inside out. Weak to fire and explosions. "It's hard to beat someone whose only boundary is their imagination."

**What he does.** He calls Kage a pawn and nearly kills him. Then he apologizes, pats his head, brings him clothes and makeup, calls Thanatos weak, gives him a "test" (kidnap Lucifer), lingers over the unconscious Lucifer, lets Kage enter the tournament, bets on him, and says "He promised me he'd win. I'm holding him to it." He also says "I'll kill you if you do" about every forty pages.

**Mysteries.**

1. Kage's hand "curled around nothing" every time Frostbite is near, and once when Anubis said his name.
2. "The other half, Frostbite would reveal to Kage later."
3. "I don't care what he…" followed by "the guy in the chair."
4. He uses Lucifer's exact insult: "sticky fucking freak."
5. He "crouched beside [Lucifer] longer than required. He reached towards Lucifer's face with his bare hand, then pulled it away."

**What makes him work.** He's the book's most contradictory adult. A murderer who brings a grieving kid eyeliner. His care is clumsy, threatening, and real.

**Prediction.** See Theories. I think Frostbite caused the convenience store attack, and his kindness to Kage is guilt. I also think he has a personal connection to Lucifer.

**Rating: 9.5/10.** The character I most want to understand.

## Nerezza

**At a glance.** A woman in black with a mask. Vain ("How dare you ruin my beautiful face"). Lives in a crappy apartment in Russia. Drinks coffee. Watches the news, and children screaming. Flirts with Thanatos. *Nerezza* is Italian for "blackness."

**Power: Umbrakinesis.** She controls shadows as parts of her body, sees in the dark, moves silently, and travels through shadows. Her tentacles can be sharp, can have toothed suckers, and can come out of other people's shadows. Limits: she can only go twenty miles from places she has walked. Overuse blinds her eyes, locks her hands, and floods her mind with pain. Too many jumps and the shadows "deny entry." Light weakens her.

**What she does.** She assassinates an official who was pushing the Security Accords and destroys a camera. She tortures Kage and Orion, kills Abby, makes Orion choose, breaks all of Kage's limbs in a game, and hands him to Thanatos "in near perfect condition." Then she rewatches his screams on TV.

**Key lines.**

- "I see why Thanatos likes you."
- "'Sticky Situation! No longer bringing smiles!'"
- "Run, boy."
- "Woops! My finger slipped!"
- "Children screaming in pain is next level television."

**What makes her work.** She's pure menace with specific limits. She's the villain the book uses to make its strongest point: she watches children suffer for entertainment, and so, in a cleaner way, does the stadium.

**What I'd watch.** She "chose" Kage to pay for Abby's explosion, and she was stopped from killing him ("The only reason you're still alive is because-!"). Her history with Kage isn't finished, and Orion's secret is hers to expose.

**Prediction.** She's the one who reveals Orion's choice, and she'll do it in a way designed to hurt both of them.

**Rating: 9/10.** Genuinely frightening.

## "The Guy in the Chair" (the boss)

**At a glance.** Unseen. Frostbite fears what he "may do." He sends Thanatos to Russia and Nerezza after politicians. He "could always use extra info" on Anubis. His organization opposes the Security Accords. Someone is keeping Kage alive ("I'm not sure if I can kill you yet").

**Prediction.** "In the chair" could mean a wheelchair, a throne, or a prison. I'd bet he has a personal interest in Kage, which is why Thanatos "needs" him, why Nerezza can't kill him, and why Frostbite was going to say "I don't care what he—" before catching himself.

# Minor and Unseen Characters

## C.O.R.E.

Rhatella's AI. It's protective, monotone, and occasionally petty. It sighs at Kage and kicks him with a giant boot. It took Kage's blood. It tells Orion it "detect[s] no hostility." It's a great comic device that could become a plot device. **Rating: 7.5/10.**

## The soul up above

The person crushed under the awning at the convenience store. Never named. Kage was holding their wrist. Kage's entire heroism is a debt to them: "I need that soul up above looking down and knowing… that I became a real hero." Cheyenne mixes them up with Abby when she sees the whistle, and in a sense, now there are two souls up above. **Prediction:** giving this person a name, a face, or a family later would be devastating, in a good way.

## Abby's brother

He was "incredibly sick." He made a gold whistle only Abby can hear. He died because Abby was told to wait. He's the reason Abby never waits. **Wish:** give him one specific detail.

## Lance's father

A high-earning villain in Quebec who taught his son to steal. Arrested. The only time he spent with Lance was during crimes. **Wish:** let him be alive and reachable.

## Alexander's mother

"You can do it sweetie!!! I'm so proud of you for trying!" She forces her son to stay and make friends. She covers his eyes, then joins his chant: "YEAH, KAGE! BEAT THAT DEVIL!" She's the only unconditionally supportive parent in the book, and Kage stares at her. **Rating: 9/10** for two lines.

## Kelly's wife and son

The wife yells at Kelly to hurry. The son "didn't end up showing up." They're seeds.

## The murdered official

A man in a suit outside a government building, crushed under Nerezza's foot. He was pushing the Security Accords. His replacement is next.

## The drunk bettor

He lost $300 on "demon boy" to a man whose ice wouldn't melt. "He your son or some shit?" It's a funny line, and given everything in this book about fathers, a pointed one.

## The crowd

The crowd is a character. It boos, cheers, chants "Nasser," "Lucifer," "Knight Volkan," and "Kage," and yells "KEEP GOING! THIS IS AWESOME!" while two boys bleed. It's the thing Kage wants "forever." It's also the thing Alex turns, with one laugh, from bloodthirsty to supportive. The book is smart to treat it as fickle, powerful, and easily led.


# Themes

## 1. Being seen versus saving people

This is the book's central argument, covered in the Verdict. Every major adult takes a side. Anubis (watched): cameras, merch, TV, "THAT IS HOW YOU PUT ON A SHOW!" Huoyan (saving): "I teach all heroes, not just ones who punch well… Pacifists make great rescue heroes." Nerezza takes the watching side to its extreme: "Children screaming in pain is next level television." Kage holds both positions at once, and the biomass is tipping him toward the wrong one.

## 2. Holding on and letting go

The book's founding image is a hand closing on a wrist and then on air. Look at how often hands carry the story:

- "His fingers were holding air." (Prologue)
- "This time, he made sure his grip held." (S1 Ch2)
- He hugs Ailani "like he could hold on to the version of them that was already gone." (S1 Ch3)
- His hand twitches when he meets Thanatos, and then he shakes that hand. (S1 Ch12)
- "His hand curled around nothing" near Frostbite. (S1 Ch13, S2 Ch10, S2 Ch15)
- Abby's hands heal his knuckles. (S1 Ch1)
- He clutches the whistle in nearly every scene of Sections 3 and 4.
- His hand presses against the glass for Severine, then pulls away. (S3 Ch2)
- His hands move toward the glass when he sees Alex's mother. (S3 Ch7)
- He breaks his own hands over and over against Abaddon. (S3 Ch16)
- Ailani presses her hand to the glass for him. (S3 Ch15)
- "You let go of my hand to ogle at a cake?… I won't let go a second time!" (S4 Ch1)
- Orion raises the whistle in a closed fist. (S3 Ch16)

The phrase "his hand" appears 99 times in the manuscript. I don't think that's an accident. Hands are where this book keeps its feelings.

## 3. Adhesion and attachment

Kage's power is to make things stick, and he's someone who can't stay attached. He "stopped unpacking his bag." He dated nine women in a few months. He tells Orion they're "not friends" and Inazuma's comments should "slide off." The book plays with this constantly, from the narration's "Adhering to every rule" to Cheyenne's "oil and water." His arc is learning to stick to people, and the tragedy is that he's learning it from villains first.

## 4. Atonement

Almost everyone is paying off a debt:

- **Kage**: the store, then Abby. "It's the only way to make up for the things I've done."
- **Orion**: the dome. "A part of him prayed for this."
- **Abby**: her brother. "She never tried, and that's what's been killing her."
- **Lance**: his father. "I try to hold people to the standard I didn't hold to myself."
- **Lucifer**: Abaddon, and whatever happened to Elias.
- **Elias**: possibly failing to stop the demon the first time.
- **Frostbite**: "the other half," which I suspect is the store.
- **Huoyan**: whatever he and Anubis were, and whatever he "hasn't told."

The book is careful not to suggest any of these debts can be paid by winning a tournament, even though most of these characters are trying exactly that.

## 5. Consent and bodies

The book returns again and again to bodies being used without permission. C.O.R.E. takes blood. Anubis photographs students and wants cameras in their rooms. Thanatos puts himself into Kage, and Frostbite says it out loud: "You just invaded the boy's body without consent and forced yourself onto him." Abaddon lives in Lucifer's body. Nerezza turns Abby's own shadow into the weapon that kills her. The Security Accords would put cameras everywhere. This is a quietly serious theme, and it gives the surveillance politics real stakes.

## 6. Secrets and masks

Almost everyone in the book is hiding something:

- Kage: the villains, the biomass, Abby's death.
- Orion: the dome, Kage's villain ties, his crush.
- Lucifer: Abaddon, the deal.
- Elias: the history.
- Huoyan: "I haven't told Anubis everything."
- Anubis: the deal, knowing Kage was alive, knowing about Abaddon, the drone.
- Frostbite: "the other half."
- Gene: her real reasons.
- Reina is the one person nobody tells anything.

Chapter titles like "What's Hiding Underneath," "The Whole Truth," and "Paid In Exposure" make this explicit. Section 4 is when the secrets start leaking: Kage's "successor" slip, Lucifer's confession, and Elias's history.

## 7. "We're not friends"

At least eight times, someone denies a friendship the book has clearly shown: Kage and Orion (many times), Abby's "I'm happy to not be your friend, Lucifer," Lucifer's "I'm not your friend!", Kage on Inazuma, Kage on Severine, and Orion on Rebento's taunt. Denying friendship is how these characters protect themselves. The best moments in the book are when the denial cracks: Kage carrying Orion to the nurse, Orion raising the whistle, and Elias hugging Kage after refusing to trust him.

## 8. Support and its dangers

Abby's purpose is "support." Her death makes the word unbearable for Kage, which is why Zulfiya's forfeit sets him off. Kage starts the book refusing help ("You may not even be there at all!") and announcing he'll make other heroes unnecessary. Abby dies partly because he wouldn't call her. By Section 3, he's accepting help from Lance and Rebento: "I should probably get used to working with others." But under pressure he slides back: "I'm not trying to befriend everybody." Lucifer's line belongs here too: "The best doesn't need support!"

## 9. Royalty, titles, and names

King Kage, the prince, Knight Volkan, "your majesty," "Sir Lance," "your kingship." Each title marks a stage in how Kage sees himself and how others see him. The King speech is ambition. The prince is Cheyenne's kindness, a smaller crown. Lance's knighting of Volkan makes Lance a kind of king.

Hero names are part of this: Sticky Situation, Hermes, Sugar Bomb, Hellfeather, Anubis, Shifting Horror, Frostbite. Anubis says, "Ours are kind of boring." Huoyan: "They're our identity now… You're the brand and merchandise guy." Kage's name is meant to make people laugh. Anubis's name is printed on an action figure in his office. Those are two different ideas of what a name is for.

## 10. Speed and time

Speed is everywhere: Orion's speed, Inazuma's "predestined" speed, Volkan's snail's pace, Elias stopping time, Huoyan's math about training time, Kage's rush ("I don't have time to learn the nice, slow, and easy way"), and Thanatos: "animals that never stop running don't evolve. They die." Chapter titles join in: "The Race," "Faster Than Fast," "Gone In An Instant," "Honoring A Snail's Pace," "Can You Outrun Explosions?", "Running Off Those Calories." The book keeps asking whether growth can be rushed. So far the answer is that you can rush it, but the bill comes later, and something else gets to collect.

## 11. Hunger

Kage literally needs to eat to use his power: cheese, oil, minibars, a fridge dragged to his bed. He was poor enough in first year to order a cup of water. He joined the program for "three meals a day." Thanatos describes him as "a starving animal" chasing meat, and Thanatos himself starves after the rebuild. Abaddon licks Kage's blood. Elias's harshest line is that Kage came "for food instead of the goodness in his heart." Section 4's chapter titles are about food: "Honey Toast," "Sweeter Than Sugar," "Running Off Those Calories," "Dinner Needs A Show." Hunger is the book's metaphor for ambition, and it's consistent.

## 12. Light and darkness, angels and demons

Abby is an angel. Lucifer is named for a fallen angel and carries the angel of the abyss. "Angels can't befriend demons." Nerezza is darkness, and Abby's light hurts her before she dies. Kage escapes Nerezza by flying toward the moon. Orion is a comet, "starlight." Kage's name means "shadow." And Anubis, a god of death who weighs hearts against a feather, used to work alongside a man named Hellfeather. The book has a quiet religious layer: Abby's "God chose me," Severine's "blasphemous," Kage's "as the bible would put it, I wanted Abaddon."

## 13. Performance

The tournament is a stage. Ailani is a dancer who's "dying to perform." Lance gives "a show." Kage is "a thespian at heart." The crowd turns two boys into "wrestlers who finally got color on camera." Nerezza calls her killings art. Anubis says "THAT IS HOW YOU PUT ON A SHOW!" Section 4 is named after the one character whose dream is performance, and it opens with an audience (Chapter NULL) and ends with "Curtain Call." That's probably deliberate.

## 14. Parents

Kage has none, and the book makes the absence visible through other people's parents. Alex's mother cheers that she's "proud of you for trying." Gene corrects Geneki in front of millions. Anubis uses his brother as a brand. Lance's father taught him crime. Kelly's son doesn't come. Thanatos speaks "like a father." Frostbite says he's "not good with kids" and pats Kage's head. The drunk asks if Kage is the mentor's son. Kage stares at Alex's mother. He wants to ask Lance what it's like to have a father teach you something, and doesn't. Anubis notices too ("Kage doesn't have that"), and uses it.

## 15. Fire and ice

Huoyan (fire) and Frostbite (ice) are the gruff, secretly caring men on opposite sides. Lucifer is fire and Reina absorbs it. Kage's hardening draws cold from his body and gives him fever. Volkan is immune to heat. Frostbite is weak to fire and explosions, and the tournament has a fire-user and a bomb-maker. The book's temperature is always in play.

# Motifs and Recurring Objects

## The gold whistle

Made by Abby's brother. Only Abby can hear it. It's gold glitter in a backpack (S1 Ch5), then a gift (S2 Ch2). Kage doesn't use it while she's alive (S2 Ch6). Then he blows it, silent, for days (S2 Ch10). It goes on a string around his neck. It ends up bloody on the asphalt, gripped before his best line (S2 Ch12). He clutches it when someone mentions "support," when Lance tells him to calm down, and when he tells Lance he's bad at saving people. He gives it to Orion before the Lucifer fight (S3 Ch15), and loses control without it. Orion raises it alone (S3 Ch16) and returns it: "You look like you need it" (S4 Ch4).

It's the best object in the book. It's silent, so it represents a call nobody can hear, and the person it was made to call is gone.

## The dolphin backpack

Abby's bag. It held water bottles for strangers and the whistle. It's what's left of her: scorched, with one strap. Kage hugs it in the bathroom and refuses to let Frostbite take it. It hasn't appeared since S2 Ch10. Where is it now?

## Glass

The stadium's thick dome. The soundproof lobby windows. Kage's hand on the glass for Severine. His hands moving toward the glass at Alex's mother. Ailani's hand on the glass for Kage. Anubis yelling behind soundproof glass at Youseff. The convenience store's windows, which "went white and burst outward." Glass separates people who are watching from people who are suffering.

## Walls

Kage gets thrown into walls again and again: by Orion, Severine, Lucifer, Nerezza, Frostbite, Abaddon, and Rebento. By S4 Ch6, "Kage was getting tired of hitting walls." It's a running joke, and it's also the shape of his life.

## Doors

Ailani slams a door in his face (S1 Ch3, S2 Ch4). Lucifer slams one on Abby's foot. Orion jams his foot in Ailani's door. Rhatella's door opens by itself. Kage casts keys to locked doors. Thanatos kicks in Nerezza's door. The stadium doors, which Kage needs to go through first. Ailani heading for the arena door first in "Curtain Call."

## Hair

Kage's droopy "lazy" hair, cut into a cartoon shape. Hair standing on end is his telekinesis tell. He rips out a tuft in frustration. Zulfiya's healing hair. Orion's dreads, with loc rings that become ammunition. Ailani ties her hair up before she performs.

## Glasses

Anubis's sunglasses, which he lowers an inch to give a speech. Elias's glasses, made by Rhatella, broken by Kage, and missed every time Elias reaches for them. Rhatella's swirling goggles, which Orion straightens. Who wears glasses, and what it means when they come off, is a small running thread.

## Hats

Cheyenne's cowboy hat, "she never took off." She lifts it to see Kage's tears. Kage steals it and wears it. It's the stake in Kage and Lance's wager.

## Teeth

Kage's tooth, knocked out by Lucifer, spat into Lucifer's eye, and permanently gone. It's the first thing the biomass can't fix, and the first visible permanent cost of the book's violence. His grin, which Lance calls comforting, now has a gap in it.

## Food

Covered under Hunger. Honey toast, cheese, oil, minibars, the fridge, the water cup, gumballs, candy, the chocolate cake he let go of a hand for.

## Insects

Lucifer's insults: ant, worm, pebble, bug, moth, cockroach. Rebento's "little beetles." Abaddon's "a bug that needs to be squashed before he cocoons and grows wings." Lance's origin story, about saving ants from a frog. Ailani's crystal butterfly. Lucifer means them as insults, and the book keeps turning them toward transformation.

## Bowing

Kage bows to Huoyan (as an apology), to Frostbite (with reverence, which Thanatos dislikes), and to Lance. Huoyan hands Gene a mic "in a sort of genuflect." The wager: Lance will bow if Kage wins. Zulfiya bows: "your majesty." Who bows to whom tracks power and respect.

## Enhancement "tells"

Kage's hair stands up. Ailani's sigil turns ninety degrees. Rebento's eyes swirl into rainbows. Lucifer's tail wags or goes stiff. Every power has a visible sign, which is good fight-writing and good characterization.

## Power cards

Heroes get exclamatory, present-tense, broadcast-style introductions: "The girl is Abby Neosame!" Villains get flat, factual ones: "This man is the villain Shifting Horror." One card gets interrupted (Cheyenne's). Some people never get one (Alexander, Youseff, Anubis, Frostbite's real identity, the boss). It's a well-used device.

## What wobbles

None of these are serious. They're the kind of thing a careful editor would flag.

1. **Kage's gray contacts.** Frostbite gives him gray contacts to hide his blue eyes in S2 Ch10. In S2 Ch14, Ailani "stared deep into his blue eyes." He probably took them out to come home as himself, which makes sense, but the book never says so. One clause would fix it.
2. **"Missing since Tuesday… three days now"** (S2 Ch9). That's right if the scene is Thursday, and one day off if it's Wednesday, the same day as S2 Ch8. Pin the day.
3. **Abaddon's pronouns.** He speaks in the third person ("ABADDON IS NOT THAT STUPID BOY!") throughout S2 Ch12, switches to "I" in Lucifer's mindscape, and mostly uses "I" in the S3 Ch16 fight, except for his entrance line. I can read it as deliberate: after Lucifer accepts that "we are one," Abaddon stops treating himself as separate. If that's the intent, it's a great detail, and one line acknowledging it would make it unmistakable. If it isn't, pick one.
4. **Gene's reason for switching the match order.** She says she hated Anubis's deal and won't "gamble away a kid's future," but moving Kage's match first doesn't obviously protect anyone, and Huoyan says so. She doesn't answer. If it's meant to show she isn't as principled as she claims, it works. If it's meant to be a real rescue, the logic needs one more step.
5. **How Lucifer knew Kage and Ailani were talking again** (S2 Ch4). They reconciled in a hidden room on a Friday, and by Monday Lucifer had told her about Reina, "one of the few times he ever spoke to me." Who told him? This might be a future reveal, but if it isn't, it's a hole.
6. **C.O.R.E.'s "no filed record of misconduct"** for Orion (S1 Ch4), one day after a verbal warning from Huoyan. It's easily explained (warnings aren't filed), and probably not worth fixing.
7. **Severine's formality.** She's set up as formal, without contractions ("I do not date fools"), but she uses contractions often ("you're a riot," "I've talked to you"). Pick a rule.
8. **"No one else knew Kage called Orion that"** (S2 Ch14). Abby and Zulfiya both heard it in S2 Ch2. The line is fine from Zulfiya's perspective, but the narration states it as a fact.
9. **The match naming.** Gene calls Round 2 "semi-finals," the half finals "finals," and the last match "grand finals." It's consistent, just unusual. A reader who knows tournaments might stumble. One line from Gene explaining her naming would turn it into a character quirk.
10. **Formatting.** The manuscript mixes straight and curly quotation marks and apostrophes in a few places (about 30 curly apostrophes and 3 curly quote marks among about 1,900 straight ones). It's invisible in most readers, but worth normalizing before publication.

# Craft Notes

These are notes about the writing itself, as opposed to the story. They're the most fixable things in the book, and fixing them would make the biggest difference to how professional it reads.

## Voice and tone

The book has two voices. The Prologue is literary, with controlled sentences and concrete images. The chapters are bright, dialogue-heavy, and anime-flavored, with power cards, catchphrases, and all-caps shouting. Both are strong. The transition between them is abrupt, and the book could blend them more. The best chapters (S2 Ch6, S2 Ch7, S3 Ch16) do exactly that, mixing the chapter voice with the Prologue's control.

The tonal range is a strength. The book moves from Rhatella's shipping to Abby's death to Kelly's smelling salts without losing the reader. That's hard.

## The power cards

The power-card introductions are a genre device done well. Hero cards are exclamatory and present tense, like a broadcast ("The girl is Abby Neosame!"). Villain cards are flat and factual ("This man is the villain Shifting Horror."). Cheyenne's card is interrupted mid-sentence by the fight. Ailani's card is written in its own time period ("The freshman class's Ailani Shoma!"). Keep this device everywhere, keep the villain contrast, and keep holding cards back for characters whose powers are a mystery (Anubis, Youseff, Cheyenne).

## Exclamation points and capitals

The manuscript has **2,119 exclamation points** in about 104,700 words, roughly one every 50 words. There are also 664 fully capitalized words of four letters or more.

The power cards earn their exclamation points. So do Gene's announcements, Abaddon, and real screaming. But a lot of ordinary dialogue and narration also ends in "!", and when everything is loud, the loud moments lose their force.

**Suggestion:** In dialogue, keep exclamation points for shouting and real surprise. In narration, remove almost all of them. Then read the Abaddon fight again, and notice how much louder his capitals feel.

## Dialogue tag punctuation

There are about **54 places** where a dialogue tag follows a period instead of a comma. For example:

> "No, you won't." Anubis answered quickly.

should be:

> "No, you won't," Anubis answered quickly.

The rule: if the words after the quote are a speech verb (said, replied, answered, yelled, asked, whispered, responded), use a comma inside the quote, and lowercase the tag. If they're an action ("Anubis stood up."), a period is right. A search for `." ` followed by a name and a speech verb will find most of them.

## Repeated beats

Some gestures carry a lot of weight in the book:

| Word | Count |
|---|---|
| nodded | 82 |
| chuckled / chuckling | 45 |
| sighed / sigh | 48 |
| smirk | 22 |
| grin | 27 |
| giggled | 20 |
| facepalmed | 12 |
| sucked his/her teeth | 10 |

None of these are wrong, but some scenes stack three or four in a row. When everyone nods, nobody's nod means anything. Try cutting about half of the nods and chuckles, and replace some with nothing at all. Most dialogue doesn't need a gesture.

## Telling after showing

The book often shows a feeling and then names it immediately. For example, in S2 Ch14, Kage's hug shows the tension breaking, and then the narration says, "The tension in the room drained from the hug, and everyone relaxed." Or a character makes a face, and then we're told what the face means. When the showing is good (and it usually is), trust it. Cutting the naming sentence will usually make the moment land harder.

## Point of view

The book uses a roaming third-person narrator that moves between heads in a scene, sometimes within a paragraph. That's a legitimate choice, and it's how the book can show the hero lounge reacting to a fight in the same scene. But sometimes the head-hopping is fast enough to blur whose scene it is (S3 Ch4's Orion–Ailani conversation, for example). One technique: give each scene a "home" character, and only leave that head for a clear reason.

## Speeches

The book's characters give long speeches: Kage's King speech, Thanatos's offer, Elias's humiliation of Kage, Anubis's succession pitch, Abaddon in the mindscape, Lance's backstory. Most of them are excellent. But several run a third longer than they need to. The best ones (Lance's ants and frog) work because they're specific and short. Try cutting each long speech by a third, and keep every line that's specific.

## Dialogue voices

The comic and loud characters (Kage, Rhatella, Rebento, Inazuma, Cheyenne, Gene, Abaddon) have distinct voices. The articulate characters (Orion, Lance, Elias, sometimes Huoyan and Anubis) can sound alike: full sentences, careful logic, formal diction. Give each a verbal habit. Elias already has one (he's literal and precise). Lance could be more old-fashioned, as Kage teases. Orion could be shorter and drier. Huoyan has ESL grammar, which Anubis corrects; use it occasionally in his dialogue.

## Chapter titles

They're good: puns ("Rhatella In A Novella," "Anubis-pheric Pressure," "Rhat Tech"), quiet ironies ("A Beautiful Past," "Honor Is For Suckers"), and structural jokes ("Chapter NULL"). A few could be sharper. "Which Kage Is Best?" promises more than its chapter delivers. "Honor Is For Suckers" could point more at Reina. But overall, they're a strength.

## Pacing

Sections 1 and 2 are well paced. Section 3's first round is deliberately anticlimactic, and the book uses the gaps between matches for character work, which mostly succeeds. If you want to tighten it, cut the hero-lounge banter in S3 Ch1 and S3 Ch3 by about a quarter. Section 4 is moving well.
